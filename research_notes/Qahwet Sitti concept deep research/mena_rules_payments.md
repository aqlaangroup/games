# MENA content regulation, cultural sensitivities, monetization rules and payments for "Qahwet Sitti" (KSA, UAE, Egypt, Jordan)

Research date: 2026-10-30. Method note: in this session, web page fetching was blocked for most outside domains (for example salmangul.com and gamegrowthadvisor.com did not resolve). Findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Treat specific numbers as "reported by" the cited source and re-verify against primary texts (Gmedia, NMA, SDAIA, ETA, Apple/Google consoles) before launch. Many sources are vendor or agency blogs; this is flagged where relevant.

## 1. Age rating and content rules (Saudi Gmedia/GCAM, UAE NMA/Media Council, Egypt, Jordan): what gets rejected or age-gated

### Takeaway
Saudi Arabia and the UAE both use 3/7/12/16/18/21 rating tiers. In Saudi Arabia a title the regulator refuses to rate is effectively banned unless the offending content is removed for that market. Commonly reported triggers are sexual content and "sexualised clothing", homosexuality, alcohol and drugs, magic, religious or political extremism, and simulated gambling. A clean-romance café game with marriage-oriented courtship should fit within 12+ or lower if it avoids kissing, revealing outfits, alcohol, same-sex romance and casino imagery. No official public criteria document was retrieved in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **Saudi regulator naming:** the former GCAM is now the General Authority for Media Regulation, branded "Gmedia". It runs an "Age rating for video games" e-service. — [Wikipedia: General Authority of Media Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Authority_of_Media_Regulation); [Gmedia e-service](https://gmedia.gov.sa/en/services/age-rating-for-video-games)
- **Saudi tiers:** the tiers are 3, 7, 12, 16, 18 and 21, and the 21 tier was added in 2025. One 2026 industry guide also says the system became "IARC-linked in 2026". That guide is secondary and unverified. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Commission_for_Audiovisual_Media); [salmangul.com GCC age-rating guide (2026)](https://salmangul.com/gaming-regulation-age-rating-gcc/)
- **Unrated means banned in Saudi Arabia:** the regulator "effectively bans any title it refuses to rate, unless publishers remove the violating content for that market". One industry guide says every game must be rated by law. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Commission_for_Audiovisual_Media); [salmangul.com](https://salmangul.com/gaming-regulation-age-rating-gcc/)
- **Saudi submission process:** the developer, publisher or local agent submits the game with gameplay videos of at least 30 minutes each, covering characters, levels and storyline. Review takes 2–10 working days. — [Gmedia e-service page, via search summary](https://gmedia.gov.sa/en/services/age-rating-for-video-games)
- **Saudi classification criteria:** reported criteria are violence, drug and alcohol use, sexual content and sexualised clothing, homosexuality, depictions of magic, and religious or politically extremist content. This comes from a user-edited wiki, so it is not authoritative. — [Nintendo fandom wiki: GCAM](https://nintendo.fandom.com/wiki/General_Commission_for_Audiovisual_Media)
- **UAE tiers and scope:** the tiers are 3, 7, 12, 16, 18 and 21. They apply to games on console, PC, online platforms and mobile phones. Simulated gambling is a listed classifiable element. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/uae-adopts-new-classification-systems-for-all-films-books-and-games-1.706479); [Gulf News: decoding UAE age ratings](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/decoding-uae-age-ratings-for-films-books-and-video-games-what-parents-should-know-1.1727272240932); [NMC age classification page](https://nmc.gov.ae/en-us/Media-Center/Events/Pages/Age-Classification-System.aspx)
- **UAE regulator renamed:** the UAE Media Council became the **National Media Authority (NMA)** in January 2026. All physically released games, including code-to-content cards, must be rated by the NMA. — [Microsoft Game Publishing Guide: age ratings guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/game-publishing/concepts/age-ratings-guidance)
- **LGBT content:** The Last of Us Part II was reportedly blocked in Saudi Arabia over violence and a same-sex relationship storyline. This comes from a commercial blog with no official citation, so it is unverified. — [ar-pay.com: games banned in Arab countries](https://ar-pay.com/blog/en/articles/video-games-banned-arab-countries/)
- **LGBT apps blocked on Apple's store:** a Fight for the Future report found Saudi Arabia had the most LGBTQ+ apps blocked of any country (28). The coverage is about two years old, and it concerns apps, not games. — [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/apple-is-letting-over-150-countries-censor-lgbtq-content-in-the-app-store/)
- **Jordan:** the only notable game action found was the Telecommunications Regulatory Commission's ban on PUBG in July 2019, citing "negative effects" and citizen complaints. Reports said six more games, including Fortnite, were planned for bans, but I found no confirmation that those followed. The ban came from the telecom regulator, not a media or rating body. — [Rappler (2019)](https://www.rappler.com/technology/234764-jordan-ban-pubg-july-2019/); [Dawn (2019)](https://www.dawn.com/news/1492513); [MMOs.com (2019)](https://mmos.com/news/jordanian-telecommunications-regulatory-commission-bans-pubg-plans-to-ban-six-other-games-including-fortnite)

### Inferences
- **Mobile store distribution:** in practice, mobile apps reach these markets through the Apple and Google store questionnaires (IARC on Google Play). Gmedia's own submission matters most for physical, console or PC releases, or if the "IARC-linked 2026" claim means the IARC questionnaire now issues Gmedia ratings. This is unverified, so confirm it.
- **Design rules for Qahwet Sitti:** the safest Gulf-wide target is the 12+ band or lower. That means:
  - Romance shown through glances, conversations, shared coffee, family-sanctioned engagement and marriage, without kissing or embraces between unmarried characters.
  - Modest outfits for all characters, with no swimwear or cleavage. Hijab and non-hijab options should both be portrayed respectfully.
  - No alcohol. Café menus should stick to coffee, tea, juices and sweets.
  - No same-sex romance routes.
  - No casino, card-table or slot imagery in UI or rewards.
  - No mocking or theological portrayal of religion. Ramadan, Eid and prayer-time references should be warm and accurate.
  - No magic or fortune-telling as a story mechanic. Coffee-cup reading (fortune-telling) is culturally familiar in the Levant but sits close to the "magic" trigger, so treat it cautiously.
- **Maps:** no source was found in this session. Commonly reported practice is to avoid maps showing disputed borders (Israel/Palestine labelling, Western Sahara, Gulf naming) and to use the term "Arabian Gulf". This should be checked by the report writer as a gap.
- **Family drama:** this is generally fine. Avoid depicting violence against women or children, or honor-related violence.

### Gaps
- The official Gmedia and NMA classification criteria documents (the actual list of what maps to each tier) could not be retrieved.
- I found no reliable source specifying rules for Egypt's Supreme Council for Media Regulation on mobile games, or for Jordan's Media Commission on game ratings in 2024–2026.
- Explicit rules on maps or flags in games were not found.
- I could not confirm whether the IARC questionnaire now produces Gmedia or NMA ratings automatically.

## 2. How romance, dating, relationship and marriage depictions have fared in Saudi and Gulf stores

### Takeaway
I found no documented case of a clean romance or story game being removed from Saudi or Gulf app stores. Removals and blocks that are reported involve LGBTQ content (apps, and allegedly TLOU2 and Kingdom Come: Deliverance II). Mainstream dating apps are growing in Saudi Arabia, which suggests that heterosexual romance as a theme is not itself a store-level problem.

### Cited Findings
- **Kingdom Come: Deliverance II:** a Steam forum thread claims the game was pulled from PlayStation in Saudi Arabia over an optional gay romance scene. This is user speculation with no developer or Sony confirmation. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1771300/discussions/0/601894472784093028)
- **Dating apps in Saudi Arabia:** downloads of top dating apps reached 3.5M in 2025 and have risen every year for 5 years (Sensor Tower data). — [Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/saudi-arabia-sees-dating-boom-for-tinder-other-apps/); [Asia Business Daily (2026)](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026022315182284845); [To Vima / WSJ](https://www.tovima.com/wsj/saudi-youth-couldnt-date-openly-a-decade-ago-now-tinder-is-booming/)
- **LGBTQ apps:** Saudi Arabia blocks the most LGBTQ apps on the App Store (28). This report is about two years old. — [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/apple-is-letting-over-150-countries-censor-lgbtq-content-in-the-app-store/)

### Inferences
- The risk is concentrated on same-sex content and sexual or physical intimacy, not on courtship itself.
- A slow-burn romance framed toward engagement or marriage, with family involvement (khutba, meeting the parents), fits local norms and matches the "family drama" premise.
- Player-reviewed sensitivity is likely a bigger risk than the regulator. A Jordanian heroine alone with a man in private spaces, or a hidden relationship, could draw backlash on social media even if it is legally fine.

### Gaps
- I could not find any Arabic-language reporting of specific romance mobile games (for example Chapters or Episode-style otome games) being removed from Saudi or UAE stores. That this is absent from search results does not prove it never happened.

## 3. Loot boxes, gacha and gambling-like mechanics (KSA, UAE, Egypt), 2024–2026 changes, and Islamic finance sensitivities

### Takeaway
I found no specific loot box law in Saudi Arabia, the UAE or Egypt as of 2026. Gambling is prohibited across the Gulf, and the UAE rating system lists simulated gambling as a classifiable element. Globally, regulation is tightening: PEGI rates loot boxes 16+ from mid-2026, Australia requires a minimum M rating, and the FTC fined HoYoverse. Islamic scholarly opinion (Islamweb fatwa) treats paid random loot boxes as gambling (maysir). Qahwet Sitti should avoid paid randomized rewards altogether.

### Cited Findings
- **No Saudi rule found:** the search found no Saudi loot box law, regulation or fatwa from 2025–2026. An industry guide advises "special caution" with randomized mechanics in the Gulf because gambling is prohibited, but cites no specific rule. — [salmangul.com](https://salmangul.com/gaming-regulation-age-rating-gcc/); [Promise Legal: loot box laws by jurisdiction (2025)](https://blog.promise.legal/loot-box-laws-game-developers/)
- **UAE gambling element:** UAE classification lists "Gambling (e.g. simulated gambling in video games)" as an element that affects ratings. I found no UAE loot-box-specific announcement. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/decoding-uae-age-ratings-for-films-books-and-video-games-what-parents-should-know-1.1727272240932)
- **UAE Child Digital Safety Law:** it bars platforms from allowing children access to "online commercial games" (gambling or betting), including advertising and promotion of them. — [Baker McKenzie (Jan 2026)](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/uae-issues-new-child-digital-safety-law); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/uae-introduces-new-child-digital-safety-law-what-online-platforms-need-to-know)
- **Islamweb fatwa:** Fatwa #379243 treats buying loot boxes with real money as gambling. Playing the game itself is permissible if loot box purchases are avoided. A Malaysian mufti ruling also cited calls opening random crates with in-game currency haram. These are not Saudi state rulings. — [Kitamen (Malaysia, June 2026)](https://kitamen.my/2026/06/26/loot-boxes-gacha-shariah-esports-malaysia-2026/)
- **PEGI:** games with paid loot boxes are rated PEGI 16 by default, and some are rated 18. Sources disagree on whether this starts in June or July 2026. — [This Week in Video Games](https://thisweekinvideogames.com/news/european-age-ratings-to-classify-loot-boxes-as-pegi-16-by-default/); [AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/games-loot-boxes-minimum-16-125708334.html)
- **Australia:** games with chance-based paid features (loot boxes or gacha) must be rated at least M, and simulated gambling games R18+. — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/loot-boxes-are-still-rife-in-kids-mobile-games-despite-ban-on-gambling-like-features-266226)
- **FTC and HoYoverse:** the settlement included a $20M payment and parental consent for under-16 loot-box purchases. The EU Digital Fairness Act proposal, expected in Q4 2026, targets loot boxes. — [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/loot-box-laws-game-developers/); [gamegrowthadvisor (2026)](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-30-gaming-regulation-compliance-guide-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended monetization:** use deterministic purchases only. That means fixed-content bundles, energy, coins and gems with known contents, cosmetic décor sets with exact items shown, a battle-pass or season pass, and remove-ads.
- **Merge-2 random generators:** if a generator or chest yields random items, keep it earned or free-energy-based rather than sold directly. Disclose odds anyway.
- **Avoid casino imagery and spinning wheels:** no "spin the wheel", slot reels or scratch cards, even with free spins. They look like gambling imagery and may raise the rating in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, and with PEGI and Australia if the game ships globally.
- **Marketing language:** avoid words like "luck/حظ", "bet", "jackpot" or "win big" in marketing.

### Gaps
- I found no Egyptian law or regulator position on loot boxes.
- No official Saudi Dar al-Ifta or Gmedia statement on gacha was found.

## 4. Data protection, age verification and child protection (Saudi PDPL, UAE PDPL and Child Digital Safety Law, Egypt PDPL, Jordan)

### Takeaway
The UAE Child Digital Safety Law (Decree-Law 26/2025) is the most significant new rule. It has been in force since 1 Jan 2026, with a compliance deadline of 1 Jan 2027, and covers "gaming platforms" and "smart applications", including foreign apps targeting UAE users. Saudi Arabia's PDPL and Egypt's PDPL (whose Executive Regulations were issued late 2025) both require guardian consent for children's data.

### Cited Findings
- **UAE CDS law scope and timing:** Federal Decree-Law No. 26 of 2025 took effect 1 Jan 2026, and platforms must comply by 1 Jan 2027. Its definition of "platforms" includes gaming platforms and smart applications. It applies to foreign services that target UAE users. — [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/uae-issues-new-child-digital-safety-law); [Bird & Bird (2026)](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/united-arab-emirates/regulating-the-uaes-virtual-playground-new-child-digital-safety-law); [KH Legal](https://khlegal.ae/uae-federal-decree-law-no-26-of-2025-on-child-digital-safety/)
- **UAE under-13 data:** processing requires explicit, documented and verifiable custodian consent, and consent must be easy to withdraw. Children's data cannot be used for targeted advertising or commercial profiling. — [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/uae-issues-new-child-digital-safety-law/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/uae-introduces-new-child-digital-safety-law-what-online-platforms-need-to-know)
- **UAE age verification and parental duties:** platforms must implement age verification mechanisms, and parents become legally responsible for monitoring their children. Some sources describe protective duties extending to under-18s. Details vary, so check the decree text. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/safety-security/uaes-new-child-digital-safety-law-now-makes-parents-legally-responsible-for-childrens-online-activity-1.500417340); [JPost](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-884604)
- **UAE PDPL:** obligations under the UAE PDPL run in parallel with the Child Digital Safety Law for children's data. — [KH Legal](https://khlegal.ae/uae-federal-decree-law-no-26-of-2025-on-child-digital-safety/)
- **Saudi PDPL regulator and minors:** SDAIA is the regulator. Marketing involving minors requires consent, typically from a parent or guardian. SDAIA launched a third public consultation on PDPL amendments on 27 April 2025, and I found nothing finalized as of the Chambers 2026 guide. — [Chambers Data Protection 2026 – Saudi Arabia](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/saudi-arabia)
- **SDAIA children's data policy (2020):** this policy requires guardian consent with some exceptions. It predates the PDPL regulations, so treat it as background. — [SDAIA Children and Incompetents' Data Protection Policy (2020, PDF)](https://sdaia.gov.sa/ar/SDAIA/about/Documents/Children%20and%20Incompetents%E2%80%99%20Data%20Protection%20Policy.pdf)
- **Egypt PDPL:** Law 151/2020 treats children's data as sensitive. Its Executive Regulations (Ministerial Decree 816 of 2025) were issued in Nov or Dec 2025; the date conflicts across sources. Under-15s need the guardian's explicit written consent, and ages 15–18 need consent from either the child or the guardian. — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/egypt/privacy/from-policy-to-practice-egypt-issues-executive-regulations-of-the-personal-data-protection-law); [Clyde & Co (Jan 2026)](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/01/egypt-regulatory-update-on-data-privacy); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/egypt-important-data-protection-update/)

### Inferences
- **Age gate at first launch:** add a neutral age gate at first launch. For under-13s in the UAE, or under-15s in Egypt, disable ad personalization and analytics profiling, and either get guardian consent or provide a restricted mode.
- **Audience positioning:** position the game as 13+ or 16+ in store questionnaires, which suits the romance theme, to avoid "directed at children" status.
- **Data minimization:** minimize data collection and avoid targeted ads to minors to meet the UAE ban on profiling children.
- **Purchase controls:** add spending-limit and parental-control hooks, which Apple and Google already offer through Ask to Buy and Family Link.

### Gaps
- I did not find Jordan's Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 24 of 2023) details in this session. It should be verified separately.
- I found no explicit Saudi age-verification mandate for games.
- The exact UAE age thresholds (13 vs 18) for each obligation are inconsistent across summaries.

## 5. Payments: billing mix, carrier billing, local wallets, web shops after 2025–2026 store policy changes, and VAT

### Takeaway
Apple and Google in-app billing remain mandatory for in-app digital goods in KSA, UAE, Egypt and Jordan. Google's and Apple's 2026 alternative billing and external-link programs cover the US, EU/EEA, UK, Japan, Australia and Korea, but I found no evidence they cover the Gulf. Local reach comes through:
- **Google Play carrier billing:** STC, Mobily, Zain and Virgin in KSA; Etisalat (e&) and others in Egypt.
- **Mada-backed cards and Apple Pay / Google Pay** as the store's funding method.
- **Web shops or top-up sites with local methods:** Mada, STC Pay, Fawry, Vodafone Cash. These are allowed only outside the app, with no in-app linking.
VAT/sales tax on digital services: Egypt 14% and Jordan 16% were confirmed in this session. KSA (15%) and UAE (5%) were not verified here.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Play carrier billing in Saudi Arabia:** STC, Mobily, Zain and Virgin participate. The charge appears on the carrier account after about 15 minutes. — [Google Play Help: payment methods – Saudi Arabia](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2651410?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DSA)
- **Google Pay in Saudi Arabia:** launched September 2025 on the mada network. — [gamegrowthadvisor MENA guide (May 2026)](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-05-28-mena-gaming-market-entry-guide-2026/)
- **STC direct carrier billing for game top-ups:** an example is Garena Free Fire diamonds, bought with an stc number plus OTP. — [stc KSA: direct billing](https://www.stc.com.sa/en/personal/lifestyle/direct-billing/garena.html)
- **Mada share:** mada dominates Saudi domestic card payments, but sources conflict on its share. One says over 70% of domestic transactions, another about 93% of card payments. — [Wikipedia: Saudi Payments Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Payments_Network); [PaymentBrief Saudi overview](https://paymentbrief.com/markets/saudi-arabia/)
- **STC Pay usage:** about 49% of Saudi players reportedly use STC Pay. This is a vendor claim with unclear methodology. STC Pay, now stc Bank, has 12M+ users. — [salmangul.com payments guide](https://salmangul.com/local-payments-carrier-billing-gaming-gcc/); [PaymentBrief](https://paymentbrief.com/markets/saudi-arabia/)
- **Carrier billing caps:** carrier billing has spending caps. Google flags this for the UAE, and the guide advises low-priced starter packs. — [salmangul.com IAP pricing guide](https://salmangul.com/in-app-purchase-monetization-gcc/)
- **Egypt cash and wallets:** Fawry Cash holds about 55% of Egypt's cash payment market. Xsolla added Fawry and Vodafone and Orange wallets for games in March 2025. — [Xsolla newsroom](https://xsolla.com/newsroom/xsolla-expands-local-payment-options-to-the-mena-region-to-reach-more-players-and-boost-revenue-in-top-growing-markets)

- **Egypt carrier billing:** Etisalat Egypt launched Google Play direct carrier billing with Bango and TPAY in May 2023. — [Seeking Alpha PR (2023)](https://seekingalpha.com/pr/19328753-bango-tpay-and-etisalat-egypt-launch-direct-carrier-billing-with-google-to-enable-in-app)
- **Google billing choice program (2026):** available in the UK and EEA from 30 June 2026, and in Japan and Australia from 30 Sep 2026. The US has its own alternative billing. Billing fee is 5% in the US, UK and EEA. The apps and games programs are available in AU, EEA, JP, UK and US from 30 Sep 2026. No Gulf country is listed. — [Play Console Help: billing choice program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17161464?hl=en); [Android Developers Blog (June 2026)](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html); [Play Console: external payments program](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16787536?hl=en)
- **Apple outside the US, EU and UK:** in Saudi Arabia, standard rules apply to digital goods. Store billing is required, and in-app links to external checkout are generally not allowed. US link-outs are allowed after the Epic ruling. EU alternative processing carries fees, but figures conflict between sources: 15% link-out vs 20%/10%. — [Dodo Payments 2026 playbook](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/digital-goods-outside-app-store); [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/alternative-payments-in-the-app-store/); [Wikipedia: Epic v. Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_Games_v._Apple)
- **Egypt VAT:** 14% on digital and remote services to Egyptian consumers. Non-residents register under the Simplified Vendor Registration Regime once B2C sales exceed EGP 500,000 in 12 months (effective 22 June 2023). B2B is handled by reverse charge. — [ETA guidelines (PDF, 2023)](https://www.eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2023-03/digital-service-guide-english.pdf); [vatcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/egypt/egypt-vat-on-digital-services-foreign-providers/); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/vatlive/country-guides/africa-and-middle-east/egypt-vat/egyptian-vat-digital-services.html)
- **Jordan sales tax:** general sales tax is 16%. A non-resident digital services registration regime has run since the start of 2024. Sources conflict on a JOD 30,000 threshold, and some telecom-related digital services carry a 24% special tax. — [PwC Tax Summaries: Jordan](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/jordan/corporate/other-taxes); [vatcalc: Jordan GST on digital services](https://www.vatcalc.com/jordan/jordan-gst-on-digital-services-update/)

### Inferences
- **Store billing and tax:** in-app purchases must go through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing in all four countries. When selling through the stores, Apple and Google generally collect and remit VAT as the merchant/marketplace, so the developer mainly worries about VAT on its own web shop.
- **Web shop:** a web shop (for example via Xsolla, Appcharge or Coda) can sell to MENA players with mada, STC Pay, Apple Pay, Fawry and Vodafone Cash. Without US, EU or UK program eligibility, though, the app cannot link or steer to it from inside the app. Promotion would need out-of-app channels such as social media, email or community. The web shop then triggers local VAT registration (Egypt above EGP 500k, Jordan regime, KSA and UAE rules).
- **Starter packs:** keep starter packs low enough to fit carrier billing caps and prepaid balances, especially in Egypt and Jordan.

### Gaps
- No verified data on Apple, Google, carrier, mada and STC Pay shares of mobile game IAP spend in KSA, UAE or Egypt. Niko Partners, Coda and Centili reports were not retrievable.
- Saudi VAT (commonly cited as 15%) and UAE VAT (commonly cited as 5%) on digital services were not confirmed by a source in this session. They need verification via ZATCA and the UAE FTA.
- Apple Pay share in the UAE and KSA was not found.
- Jordan-specific payment methods were not found. Commonly mentioned options, such as eFAWATEERcom, CliQ, Orange Money and Zain Cash, are unverified here.
- Whether Apple's or Google's 2026 programs have expanded to any MENA market is unknown. The Play Console "over 35 eligible countries" list was not visible.

## 6. Local pricing norms for IAP (SAR, AED, EGP, JOD)

### Takeaway
Industry guidance says to set deliberate local-currency tiers rather than auto-convert from USD:
- **UAE:** tolerates premium bundles.
- **Saudi Arabia:** similar to the UAE in spending.
- **Egypt:** needs much lower entry points and smaller denominations. EA's FC Mobile Egypt web store starts at 19.99 EGP and tops out at 4,999.99 EGP.
I found no verified SAR, AED or JOD tier tables.

### Cited Findings
- **Set local tiers:** guides recommend setting SAR, AED and EGP tiers deliberately instead of auto-converting, noting that Egypt needs much lower entry offers than the Gulf. — [gamegrowthadvisor (2026)](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-05-28-mena-gaming-market-entry-guide-2026/)
- **Psychological price points:** high-ARPU UAE tolerates premium pricing and high-end bundles. Egypt is a volume market that needs accessible entry points. Guides advise showing SAR and AED at psychologically sensible local price points. — [salmangul.com IAP guide](https://salmangul.com/in-app-purchase-monetization-gcc/)
- **EA Sports FC Mobile Egypt store:** packs run from 19.99 EGP (40 FC Points) to 4,999.99 EGP (20,000 points). — [EA FC Mobile store (Egypt)](https://store.fcm.ea.com/en-eg/easports-fcmobile)
- **Global benchmark:** the median top-IAP price in top-grossing US App Store games was $9.99 every year from 2019 to 2026. This is a US benchmark only. — [illuscaden monetization tracker](https://illuscaden.com/mobile-game-monetization-tracker/)

### Inferences
- **KSA and UAE:** offer roughly USD-equivalent ladders, from about $1 to $100. Lean on the store's local tiers ending in .99, and use bundles and a season pass at mid tiers.
- **Egypt:** create Egypt-specific cheaper SKUs or rely on Apple and Google regional price suggestions. Entry offers should sit in the tens of EGP, consistent with EA's 19.99 EGP floor.
- **Jordan:** a mid-income market. Jordan's JOD is strong (about 1.41 USD), but purchasing power is lower than in the Gulf, so price nearer to Egypt-plus than to Gulf levels. This is an inference and needs data.
- **Apple and Google price tables:** let the stores' auto-generated local prices serve as the baseline, then override for Egypt and Jordan.

### Gaps
- No verified Apple or Google tier values in SAR, AED, EGP or JOD were retrieved. Check the App Store Connect and Play Console price tables.
- No Jordan-specific IAP pricing data was found.
- No ARPPU or price-elasticity data by country was found.
