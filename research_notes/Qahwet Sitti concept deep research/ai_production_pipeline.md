## 1. Which casual/mobile studios publicly use generative AI, and what savings did they report?

### Takeaway
Large casual/mobile publishers (Rovio, Playtika, Playrix, King, Goodgame) use generative AI mainly for **backgrounds, 2D asset variants, LiveOps/season art and level generation**, and keep **characters and brand IP human-made**. The reported savings are 50–80% of production time on specific asset types. No studio has published a hard cost or headcount figure. King is the most cited example of AI-linked headcount cuts, and that story rests on anonymous sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Rovio (Angry Birds Dream Blast, 2025):** A write-up of an AWS re:Invent 2025 talk says Rovio's ML team built an internal toolset, "Beacon Picasso", using models fine-tuned on Amazon SageMaker/Bedrock. It reports that **Season Pass background production time fell by 80% (4 days instead of 20)**. This is a secondary write-up of the talk, not fetched directly. — [zenn.dev summary of re:Invent 2025 talk](https://zenn.dev/kiiwami/articles/ab05fb630838f937?locale=en)
- Rovio says it uses AI **only for background creation** in Angry Birds Dream Blast and does not use it for core brand elements such as characters. — [Music x Media session: "Art, AI & Angry Birds"](https://musicmedia.fi/en/program/art-ai-angry-birds-delivering-on-the-brand-promise-in-the-era-of-generative-ai-tools/)
- **Playtika:** Built an internal generative AI platform for art production across its studios, with a stated **goal of cutting art production time by 50%**. It fine-tunes and deploys diffusion models (SD 1.5, then SDXL) at scale. The 50% is a target, not a reported outcome, and the source is a secondary database summary. — [ZenML LLMOps Database](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/production-scale-generative-ai-infrastructure-for-game-art-creation)
- **Playrix** (Gardenscapes/Homescapes/Township, the closest genre analog: decorate/makeover + puzzle): job postings describe a cross-project team that brings generative AI into the art pipeline, mainly with **FLUX models fine-tuned via LoRA/DreamBooth to match house style**. - BCG (2024): 68% of studios expect to realize savings from GenAI. — [BCG (2024)](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2024/could-genai-be-gamings-ultimate-power-up)

### Inferences
- The defensible pattern for Qahwet Sitti matches Rovio's and Playrix's: **humans own the characters (Sitti, the family, café regulars), the hero room key art and the style guide**. AI fine-tuned on that house style (LoRA/custom model) handles **merge-chain item variants, café room backgrounds/props for makeover sets, seasonal reskins of event art, and marketing creative**. In merge-2 games, item chains (often 8–12 tiers × dozens of chains) and room decor sets are the largest asset volume, so they offer the biggest savings.
- Realistic savings to plan for are **50–80% time reduction on backgrounds and variants** (Rovio's measured 80%, Playtika's 50% target), not on characters. Headcount savings show up mainly as fewer outsourced art hours and smaller LiveOps art teams, not as no artist at all.
- The King case shows AI also targets **level design and narrative drafting**. For a merge game, the analog is procedural or AI-assisted order/board balancing and LLM-drafted dialogue that a human writer then edits.

### Gaps
- No casual studio has published audited cost (USD) or headcount savings from AI art. All figures are time-based, targets, or vendor claims.
- I found no public AI-use statement from merge-genre leaders (Metacore/Supercell, Microfun/Gossip Harbor, Magmatic/Travel Town, Moon Active).
- Could not directly verify the Rovio 80% figure against the original AWS session video or slides (fetch blocked).
- I found no public studio case study on **AI narrative drafting** or **AI-generated event content** in casual games with quantified results, apart from the King reporting.

## 2. Tools commonly used and their quality for Arabic text and Arabic dialect voice

### Takeaway
Studios run **fine-tuned diffusion models** (SDXL/FLUX + LoRA, via in-house platforms or SaaS such as Layer and Scenario) for style-consistent 2D art. For voice, ElevenLabs is the default TTS. **Arabic dialect quality is the weak point.** Vendor catalogs list Saudi and Egyptian voices, but user reports show dialect drift toward MSA or the wrong dialect, and I found no independent benchmark of dialect TTS quality. LLMs translate into Arabic dialects reasonably well in academic tests, but dialect diversity remains hard and native review is required.

### Cited Findings
- **Art tools used in production:** Playrix uses FLUX + LoRA/DreamBooth fine-tuned to house style ([Remocate](https://www.remocate.app/jobs/generative-ai-engineer)). Playtika fine-tunes SD 1.5/SDXL in-house ([ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/production-scale-generative-ai-infrastructure-for-game-art-creation)). Rovio fine-tunes on AWS SageMaker/Bedrock ([zenn.dev summary](https://zenn.dev/kiiwami/articles/ab05fb630838f937?locale=en)). Layer and Scenario are the SaaS options marketed to mobile LiveOps teams ([Layer mobile games](https://layer.ai/industries/mobile-games); [Scenario case studies](https://www.scenario.com/case-studies)).
- **ElevenLabs Arabic, Saudi:** One reseller lists dozens of ElevenLabs Arabic voices tagged with a Saudi accent. This is a catalog listing, not a quality test. — [Famulor](https://www.famulor.io/voices/elevenlabs/languages/ar/accents/saudi)
- **ElevenLabs Arabic, Egyptian:** ElevenLabs markets "Egyptian accent text to speech" with "genuine Egyptian accents" **[VENDOR CLAIM]**. — [ElevenLabs blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/egyptian-accent-text-to-speech); ElevenLabs calls itself "the premier choice" for Arabic TTS **[VENDOR CLAIM]** — [ElevenLabs blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/best-arabic-text-to-speech)
- **Levantine dialect drift (user report):** A Retell AI developer serving Levant clients reported that a custom ElevenLabs voice cloned from a Lebanese speaker **spoke Saudi Arabic instead of Lebanese** after deployment. — [Retell AI community](https://community.retellai.com/t/arabic-language-voice-agent-the-client-base-is-mainly-in-the-levant-region-so-i-need-support-for-lebanese-accent/1452)
- **MSA drift (user report, Jan 2025):** A Vapi user said Arabic output sounded like formal High Arabic or was Egyptian when Saudi/Gulf was wanted. The provider and configuration are unclear. — [Vapi support thread](https://support.vapi.ai/t/27085893/arabic-dialects)
- **Dialect TTS remains a research problem:** A 2026 AbjadNLP paper proposes LLM-generated synthetic data pipelines to train dialectal Arabic TTS models, which implies off-the-shelf dialect TTS is still limited. — [ACL Anthology: LLM-to-Speech](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2026.abjadnlp-1.6); also [arXiv 2602.15675](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15675)
- **LLM translation into Arabic dialects:** A Sept 2025 Frontiers study compared GPT models and Gemini across 15+ Arabic dialects. TER scores of 6–25% indicate generally good quality, and GPT models (especially GPT-5) handled Levantine better than Gemini. — [Frontiers in AI (2025)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2025.1661789/full)
- A 2026 AbjadNLP paper benchmarking English-to-16-dialect translation highlights "the difficulty of handling dialectal diversity" and possible training data contamination. — [ACL Anthology (2026)](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2026.abjadnlp-1.41)
- **Game localization with LLMs:** The 2025 *Black Myth: Wukong* case study found that automatic metrics and MQM may be inadequate for judging creative game translation, so human evaluation is needed — [ACL Anthology LM4DH 2025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.lm4dh-1.16). An AMTA 2026 paper on game LQA across 8 LLMs found closed models best overall, with open-weight Qwen-2.5-72B competitive at much lower cost — [ACL Anthology AMTA 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.amta-research.12/)
- **SauDial dataset:** A Saudi dialect game-localization dataset, generated with GPT-4o and refined by native-speaker validation. It is usable as a reference or eval set. — [Mendeley Data](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/mzdwkb2t6d/2)

### Inferences
- For Qahwet Sitti ("Sitti" suggests a Levantine or Egyptian grandmother voice), **do not assume TTS will hold a specific dialect**. Plan a voice test: 20–30 lines per candidate voice (ElevenLabs plus 1–2 alternatives), scored by native speakers of the target dialect, before committing.
- Lowest-risk voice pipeline: **hire a native dialect voice actor under a written consent/licensing contract and have them record the hero lines** (Sitti's catchphrases, story beats). TTS or a consented voice clone of that same actor can then fill the long tail (barks, event lines, LiveOps). This matches what Embark backed into (see Q4).
- Narrative pipeline: an LLM drafts dialogue in dialect or MSA, then a **native dialect writer edits**. The Frontiers study suggests GPT-class models are good enough as a first draft, and the Wukong paper shows humans must judge creative quality.
- Arabic text rendering *inside generated images* (signage, menus in the café) is a known diffusion-model weakness. Set Arabic text as a separate typography layer in the engine and never bake it into AI images. This is an inference from general model behavior; I found no source in this session that tested it.

### Gaps
- No independent 2025–2026 benchmark of ElevenLabs, Inworld, Azure, Google or Arabic-native vendors (e.g., Nabarati) for **Levantine, Egyptian or Gulf dialect TTS naturalness**.
- I found no data on Inworld or Ludo.ai Arabic quality.
- I did not test Arabic glyph rendering in Midjourney, FLUX or Scenario directly.

## 3. Typical team size and cost for a casual merge game: prototype, soft launch, LiveOps; outsourced art per item/room

### Takeaway
Public data is thin and mostly from outsourcing vendors. Casual titles are quoted at **$30K–80K** to build at the low end, and 2D art outsourcing at **$25–80/hour**. Merge-genre leaders prototype with **2–3 people**, then scale to large LiveOps orgs: Metacore grew from about 15 to 170–250 staff, then was cut back. Post-launch spending dominates the budget.

### Cited Findings
- **Metacore (Merge Mansion):** About 15 people at the 2020 launch, 170+ by 2021, later reported at 200–250+. It **prototypes in 2–3 person teams** and expands only after player data validates the concept. — [Metacore press release](https://metacoregames.com/press-release/merge-mansion-developer-metacore-expands-to-berlin-as-merge-mansion-hits-50m-downloads/); [Rise and Play](https://riseandplay.substack.com/p/109-how-metacore-disrupted-the-puzzle-bee)
- Merge Mansion passed **$700M revenue after five years**. — [GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/business/fifth-anniversary-metacores-merge-mansion-with-more-than-dollar700-million-in-revenue-after-five-years-d637f659709480d20a95acfaaf3e5b0b)
- Metacore later laid off about 159 staff and closed its Germany and Sweden studios. Supercell is acquiring the rest of Metacore and folding Merge Mansion into its live portfolio. Dates and counts differ slightly across reports. — [GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/merge-mansion-dev-metacore-lays-off-159-employees-closes-studios-in-germany-and-sweden-r3779/); [Munich Startup](https://insights.munich-startup.de/news/feed/supercell-acquires-merge-mansion-studio-metacore-as-160-jobs-face-cuts)
- **[VENDOR ESTIMATE] Budgets:** Casual/puzzle mobile is quoted at $30K–80K, with level count as the main lever. Team size and seniority make up 55–70% of budget. 52% of a $60K game's two-year spend falls after launch. iOS CPI is about $4.22 and Android about $2.97 in 2026. — [Pixune 2026 cost breakdown](https://pixune.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-make-a-game/). A soft-launch-ready build with 3 tested mechanic variants and custom art direction lands near $40K — [Whimsy Games](https://whimsygames.co/blog/how-much-does-game-development-really-cost/)
- **[VENDOR ESTIMATE] Art outsourcing rates:** 2D concept, icon or UI work runs $25–80/hour. 2D art packages run $10K–50K. Retainers or dedicated teams cut rates 15–25% versus per-asset pricing. Add 20–30% contingency. — [Pixune art outsourcing price](https://pixune.com/blog/game-art-outsourcing-price/); [Whimsy Games art outsourcing guide](https://whimsygames.co/blog/the-complete-guide-to-game-art-outsourcing-costs-timelines-and-how-to-choose-a-studio/)
- **[VENDOR ESTIMATE] Dedicated offshore team:** $15K–40K/month. Maintenance is 15–20% of the initial budget per year. — [Whimsy Games outsourcing costs](https://whimsygames.co/blog/complete-guide-to-game-development-outsourcing-costs/)
- **LiveOps with a skeleton crew:** MobilityWare's Larry Hsieh describes running LiveOps with one or two people and a single artist, enabled by remote-config tooling. — [CleverTap interview](https://clevertap.com/blog/larry-hsieh-on-liveops-keep-the-game-relevant-with-minimal-resources/)
- Lost Lore (2023): 100 character concepts done traditionally were estimated at about $50K, or about $500 per character concept. — [Game World Observer (2023)](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/01/27/ai-use-case-how-a-mobile-game-development-studio-saved-70k-in-expenses)

### Inferences
- A lean phasing for Qahwet Sitti, inferred from the figures above:
  - **Prototype:** 2–4 people (designer/producer, engineer, artist/AD, part-time writer).
  - **Soft launch:** about 5–10 people, adding LiveOps/econ, a second artist or AI art operator, UA/analytics, and outsourced voice.
  - **LiveOps:** 8–15 core staff plus outsourcing.
  These are inferences, not sourced benchmarks.
- The $30K–80K vendor numbers likely understate a content-heavy merge + story + makeover game with voice. Merge Mansion-class competitors spend far more, and UA dominates post-launch spend.
- Per-room makeover art cost could not be sourced. A practical way to estimate it: count props per room (about 10–20 swappable items × 3 style options) and multiply by hourly rates.

### Gaps
- No sourced **per-item merge chain** or **per-room makeover** outsourcing price.
- No public team-size breakdown for Gossip Harbor, Travel Town or Seaside Escape LiveOps teams.
- No sourced Arabic dialect voice actor rates for MENA (per word or per hour).

## 4. Platform and legal risks: store AI rules, copyright, player backlash, voice actor consent and unions

### Takeaway
Mobile stores (Apple, Google) **do not require labeling of AI-made art in a game**. Their AI rules target generative-AI features (deepfakes, offensive output, UGC moderation). Steam does require disclosure of player-facing AI content. The bigger risks are **reputational**: 2025–2026 backlash cases mostly involve PC/console games and AI voice. **Copyright** cannot protect purely AI-generated assets. **Voice cloning** needs explicit written consent, and SAG-AFTRA's 2025 deal sets the norm.

### Cited Findings
- **Steam (updated Jan 2026):** Disclosure is now limited to AI-generated content players actually see or hear. Back-end efficiency tools such as coding assistants are exempt. Outlets disagree on whether the form is enforced. — [GameMeca](https://www.gamemeca.com/en/view.php?gid=1770331); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-updates-AI-disclosure-form-requiring-developers-to-report-visible-and-in-game-AI-but-not-background-tools.1206103.0.html). Epic's Tim Sweeney criticized the labels in Oct 2026, and Valve kept the policy — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Epic-CEO-Tim-Sweeney-lambasts-abhorrent-Steam-AI-Disclosure-Policy-claiming-labels-double-as-political-statement.1433682.0.html)
- **Apple (Oct 2026 revision):** Guideline 1.2 now covers AI-generated nonconsensual intimate imagery, and AI content-creation apps are treated as UGC platforms that must provide filtering, reporting and blocking. Some aggregators describe a general AI-labeling requirement, but I could not confirm that against Apple's text. — [ithinkdiff](https://www.ithinkdiff.com/app-store-rules-ai-deepfakes-nudify-apps/); [ConductAtlas change record](https://conductatlas.com/change/2026-10-17-apple-apple-app-store-review-guidelines-5687/)
- **Google Play:** The AI-Generated Content policy applies to apps whose generative AI features produce content. Developers must prevent offensive output and give users in-app reporting or flagging. — [Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14094294); [Android Developers Blog (June 2024)](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2024/06/enabling-safe-ai-experiences.html?hl=id). One legal analysis says Play does not expressly require disclosing AI use in listings, though the description must not be misleading — [Revera Legal](https://www.revera.legal/?p=3557)
- **Copyright (US):** The US Copyright Office's Part 2 report (Jan 2025) says purely AI-generated material is not copyrightable, while human selection, arrangement and modification can be protected. This comes from prior knowledge and was not re-fetched in this session. — [USCO Part 2 report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)
- **SAG-AFTRA 2025 Interactive Media Agreement (ratified July 2025, 95.04%):** Requires advance notice and **explicit written consent** for digital replicas of voice, face or movement. Uses beyond the original game need separate consent. Employers must disclose real-time speech generation. Replica creation time is paid as work time. Usage reports are required, and higher minimums apply for real-time generation. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/329479/video-game-performers-ai-consent-rules); [Graphic Policy](https://graphicpolicy.com/2025/07/10/sag-aftra-members-approve-the-2025-video-game-agreement/); [Mondaq analysis](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/gaming/1703164/sag-aftras-new-video-game-agreement)
- **Voice backlash (Arc Raiders, 2025–2026):** Embark used TTS trained on paid, consenting actors. It still drew heavy criticism, including a 2/5 Eurogamer review cited mainly for its AI use. In March 2026 the CEO said Embark had begun replacing many AI lines with human actors: "A real professional actor is better than AI." — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/arc-raiders/ai-voices-replaced); [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/03/arc-raiders-replacing-some-of-its-controversial-ai-voices-with-better-human-ones); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/eurogamer-s-2-5-arc-raiders-review-splits-the-gaming-community/)
- **Art backlash cases 2025–2026:**
  - Crimson Desert (Mar 2026): AI paintings found after launch; Pearl Abyss pledged to replace them — [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260323-crimson-desert-ai-art)
  - Postal: Bullet Paradise (Dec 2025): canceled a day after reveal over AI in its trailer — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/351204/new-postal-game-canceled-one-day-after-reveal-generative-ai-allegations)
  - Party Animals (May 2026): 500+ negative Steam reviews per day after an AI video contest — [Notebookcheck](https://notebookcheck.net/Party-Animals-hit-with-Steam-review-bombing-as-AI-slop.1299780.0.html)
  - Battlefield 6 skins (Jan 2026) — [Military.com](https://365.military.com/feature/2026/01/01/battlefield-6-players-are-furious-over-wacky-skins-and-ai-art-claims.html)
- **Mobile-specific backlash:** Activision deleted an AI-image Guitar Hero Mobile announcement after backlash, and was criticized for AI-generated mobile ads. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/activision-gets-criticized-for-ai-generated-mobile-game-advertisements/). Xbox deleted AI art promoting indie games after backlash — [GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/articles/xbox-quickly-deletes-ai-art-promoting-indies-after-backlash)
- **King layoffs** drew public criticism ("a slap to the face"). — [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/candy-crush-maker-sparks-outrage-103017927.html)

### Inferences
- For a mobile-only launch, **store compliance is low-risk** as long as the game has no player-facing generative feature. If the studio adds live LLM chat with Sitti, Apple's and Google's UGC/reporting requirements apply. A Steam/PC port would require the disclosure.
- **Reputational risk is asymmetric:** casual mobile audiences have so far been less vocal than PC core gamers, but obvious AI artifacts in hero art or in marketing get noticed. Voice is the most sensitive area. In MENA, cultural authenticity of a teta/sitti character is the core selling point, so a flat TTS grandmother could hurt the brand more than AI backgrounds would.
- **Copyright:** keep human authorship on hero characters and logos, which also protects trademark/IP value. Document human edits to AI assets. Use models with clear training or commercial terms (vendor-licensed or self-trained on owned art).
- **Voice consent:** even outside SAG-AFTRA's jurisdiction (most MENA actors are non-union), copy its terms. Get written consent for a replica, define scope (this game only vs. sequels/ads), pay for replica sessions, and disclose real-time generation.

### Gaps
- No MENA-specific law or guidance found on voice cloning or personality rights (e.g., UAE, Saudi, Jordan, Egypt). This needs local legal review.
- I could not confirm whether Apple's Oct 2026 guidelines include a general AI-content labeling rule (aggregators conflict).
- No documented 2025–2026 case of a **casual mobile game** being review-bombed for AI art.

## 5. LiveOps tooling: event templates, remote config, reusable content systems

### Takeaway
The main cost lever for LiveOps is **data-driven events**: remote config, feature flags and event calendars let designers ship and reskin events without client builds. Combined with AI-assisted reskins of a fixed event template, this is how small teams sustain an event cadence. Public quantified data is scarce and mostly vendor-authored.

### Cited Findings
- **[VENDOR] Heroic Labs Satori:** Without remote config, a LiveOps change needs code, build and store review, which takes days to weeks. With it, console edits reach clients on their next fetch. Event calendars and per-event feature-flag overrides let one event structure run with different values. — [Satori remote configuration docs](https://heroiclabs.com/docs/satori/concepts/remote-configuration/); [Satori introduction](https://heroiclabs.com/docs/satori/concepts/introduction/llm.md)
