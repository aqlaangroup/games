## 1. The season in one paragraph

Teta Im Khalil has died. She leaves her old Amman café to both her daughters, Rana and Nawal, who haven't spoken since 2006. Rana's daughter Salma comes back from Dubai to reopen it. Nawal's daughter Lana arrives from Riyadh with the other half. The cousins were inseparable as children, until the family split. Through the café's regulars, Teta's hidden letters and ten playable memories of 1974, they learn what happened in 2006: it was Teta's mistake, and Nawal kept her secret. The season ends at a neighbourhood wedding, with the two mothers drinking coffee at the same table, and the cousins planning a second café in Riyadh.

## 3. The 2006 secret

**What really happened (told only in chapter 9):**
- In 2006 the café was in debt. The chains had come to Amman, and Teta was getting older.
- The founder of a coffee chain *(Faris's father)* offered to buy Teta's knafeh recipe and the name "Knafeh Im Khalil". In public, Teta said no.
- In private, she sold him the right to use both for ten years. The money paid the debt. She was ashamed.
- Nawal worked at a bank and arranged the papers. Teta made her promise to tell no one, not even Rana. Nawal's name is on the papers as the witness.
- The chain put "Knafeh Im Khalil" in its shops. Rana saw it, found Nawal's name on the papers, and believed her sister had sold the family's recipe. Nawal had just bought a house in Riyadh, which seemed to prove it.
- Rana asked Teta. Teta, ashamed, said "ask your sister". Nawal had promised, so she said nothing. She was hurt that Rana could believe it, and she left for Riyadh.
- Teta tore the recipe page out of her book, so it could never be sold again. On its back she wrote the whole truth and signed it. She gave the page to Abu Fadi and asked him to hand it over only "when both girls' girls are under one roof".
- She paid the chain back over the years from the café's takings, until 2019.

**Why it matters now:** Faris, the founder's son, wants to buy the building (chapter 8). He thinks the old agreement gives him the first right to buy it. The back of the page shows the debt paid in full and the agreement ended, so he has no claim.

**What each person knows at the start:**

| Person | Knows |
|---|---|
| Rana | Only what she saw in 2006. She hasn't entered the café since. |
| Nawal | Everything, but she promised. |
| Abu Fadi | That the page holds a secret, not what it says. He keeps his promise to Teta and drops small hints. |
| Faris | That his father "did a deal" with Teta. Not what it cost her. |
| Salma and Lana | Nothing. Each has heard only her own mother's silence. |

**Rule for writers:** no one in the story is a villain. Rana was hurt, Nawal was loyal, Teta was proud and ashamed, and Faris is doing business. The season forgives everyone.

## 10. Voice sheets

Every line is written by a native writer in the character's dialect (`/CLAUDE.md`). These sheets describe the voice; they are not dialogue.

**Salma (urban Amman):** quick, warm, a little self-mocking. Mixes in English work words from Dubai (meeting, deadline), then catches herself. Says "هسّا" and "هون". Never cruel, even when angry; she goes quiet instead.

**Lana (Najdi, Riyadh):** precise, dry humour, proud. Uses "وش", "زين", "أبغى". Softer with Abu Fadi than with Salma. Switches to careful Ammani when she wants something from a neighbour, and gets it slightly wrong.

**Teta Im Khalil (old Amman):** proverbs, blessings and orders in one breath. Calls everyone "يا روحي". Never explains herself. In 1974 she is quick and teasing; in letters she is slow and careful.

**Abu Fadi (Amman, older):** short sentences, long pauses. Answers questions with stories. Says "يا بنتي". Keeps promises absolutely; changes the subject instead of lying.

**Khalto Samira (Amman):** loud, fast, every sentence a plan. Swears by her children. Her real feelings come out only as jokes.

**Karim (Amman, working man):** few words, all practical. Talks about wood like people. Calls Teta "الحجّة".

**Rana (Amman):** formal when hurt, warm when she forgets to be hurt. Speaks of "your Teta", never "my mother", until chapter 10.

**Nawal (Amman with Riyadh years in it):** Ammani grammar with Najdi words. Gentle, careful, says less than she knows.

**Faris (polished Amman business talk):** English business words, perfect manners. Becomes plainer Ammani as he becomes honest.

**Young Teta and young Fadi (1974 Amman):** older words, slower pace than today's characters. No modern loanwords.

## 11. Writing rules and checks

- **Rating 12+:** romance is family-centred and marriage-minded. No dating, no alcohol, no politics. Faith is present and gentle ("الله يرحمها").
- **Tap-to-explain:** every Amman word a Saudi player may not know is marked in Ink and goes in the glossary table.
- **Line length:** 3–6 lines a scene, each line short enough for the dialogue card at large text.
- **Comprehension check:** Saudi readers (`PLAN.md`, risks) read chapter 1, then chapters 2–5, before each ring ends. They list every line they had to read twice. Each such word gets a glossary entry or is rewritten.
- **Languages:** the dialect file is the source. The English and neutral-Arabic files follow it line for line (`STORY_LANGUAGES` in `missing/11.md`; the parity test checks it).
