# Test Qahwet Sitti Before Scoring It Higher

Qahwet Sitti is a strong concept, but desk research supports about **7.8/10, not 8.1**. No document can move it to 9.8. The audience fit is real. Women are **37%+ of MENA-3 gamers and rising** ([Niko Partners](https://nikopartners.com/the-future-of-gaming-in-mena-3-understanding-the-opportunity/)). Saudi women say they want casual, story-rich, mobile games ([ACM CHI PLAY 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3744736.3749348)). We found **no direct competitor** that combines an Arab heroine, merge, a café and a dialect story ([Merge Inn](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.mergegame.merge&hl=en_US); [Najd](https://thurayax.itch.io/najd)). Three things pull the score down. First, the merge market is winner-take-most: **none of 91 merge launches in H1 2025 passed $100k in monthly revenue** ([AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/casual-report-h1-2025)). Second, money is uneven: ARPU is about **$85 in the UAE, $55 in Saudi Arabia and $3.40 in Egypt** ([Niko Partners](https://nikopartners.com/the-future-of-gaming-in-mena-3-understanding-the-opportunity/)), and Jordan has no data. Third, the evidence contradicts three earlier ideas. Heavy Jordanian dialect may not travel well to the Gulf. Ramadan itself is not the best time to buy users. Arabic dialect AI voice is not reliable yet. This report lists the changes the evidence supports. It then gives a revised scorecard and a test plan. The plan names the measured result that would justify raising each sub-score. **Only real player data from a soft launch can justify a 9.8. No outside source can certify that score for an unreleased game.**

A note on the evidence. Most sources were read through search summaries, not full pages. Many numbers come from vendors, agencies or blogs. Treat single-source numbers as "reported", not proven.

## The audience is real, but the money sits in Saudi Arabia and the UAE

**Who plays.** Women are a large and growing share of players. Niko Partners puts women at **37%+ of MENA-3 gamers in 2025**, up from **32.6% in 2023** ([Niko Partners](https://nikopartners.com/the-future-of-gaming-in-mena-3-understanding-the-opportunity/)). A Saudi government figure says **48% of Saudi gamers are women**, but its year is unclear ([Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/624188)). The figures differ because they count different things. "Any mobile play" captures casual players like your audience. "Self-identified gamer" surveys miss many of them. Jordan is the weakest market on evidence. The latest data we found is from 2018, when only **38% of Jordanians** played on a phone ([Media Use in the Middle East 2018](https://www.mideastmedia.org/survey/2018/chapter/gaming/)).

**What they want.** In a survey of 247 Saudi women, **82% called themselves casual gamers**. They were "primarily driven by social connection and emotional immersion", with "a strong preference for casual, narrative-rich, and mobile games" ([ACM CHI PLAY 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3744736.3749348)). Puzzle is the #1 mobile genre in MENA-3 ([Niko Partners](https://nikopartners.com/the-future-of-gaming-in-mena-3-understanding-the-opportunity/)). The global genre leader proves the same audience pays. **Gossip Harbor's players are 79% female, average age 32** ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/gossip-harbors-meteoric-rise-in-merge/)). It is **#9 top-grossing on Google Play in Saudi Arabia** ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/stats/google-play-rankings/top_grossing/game/sa)). So Saudi women already pay for merge-plus-story games. Today they pay for games with Western heroines.

**What they avoid.** Many Saudi women **avoid voice chat and hide their identity** in games because of harassment and gatekeeping ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3744736.3749348)). One 2022 study found **68.3%** of its sample faced verbal harassment while gaming (cited in the same paper). So social features should be solo-first. Gifting and café visits work. Open chat with strangers does not.

**Where the money is.** Spending per player is very uneven. Saudi Arabia makes up about **59% of MENA-3 games spending** ([Virtwave, relaying Niko](https://www.virtwave.com/blog/middle-east-gaming-statistics-2026)). Egypt has more players but very low ARPU. Payments also differ by country. In Saudi Arabia, **Mada is used by 93% of gamers**. In Egypt, **Vodafone Cash is used by 74%**, and only **2.8%** of Egyptian gamers have a credit card ([Xsolla x Niko whitepaper](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Xsolla-Niko-Partners-What-Does-the-Monetization-Landscape-Look-Like-for-Video-Games-in-MENA.pdf)). Apple and Google billing are still required for in-app goods in all four countries. The 2026 alternative-billing programs cover the US, EU, UK, Japan, Australia and Korea. They do not cover the Gulf ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17161464?hl=en)). Google Play carrier billing works with STC, Mobily, Zain and Virgin in Saudi Arabia ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2651410?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DSA)). The practical meaning is simple. **Saudi Arabia and the UAE are your paying markets.** Egypt, and probably Jordan, are volume and ad-revenue markets. Jordan is home and story, not the main revenue source.

**Ad costs.** MENA is cheap to reach. In 2025 the MENA gaming CPM was about **$1.49**, against **$4.34** globally. The UAE CPM rose to **$3.25**, and **UAE CPI rose 54%**, while **Saudi CPI rose only 5%** ([GameDev Reports on Adjust](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report); [Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-05-28-mena-gaming-market-entry-guide-2026/)). MENA had the **highest install rate per 1,000 ads (IPM 11.56)** of any region in 2024 ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-mobile-games-insights-report)). No source gives casual-game CPI by country for Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Egypt or Jordan. Snapchat reaches **89% of Saudi adults**, and its Saudi ad audience is **43.5% female** ([BIMO](https://www.bimogroup.co/blog/snapchat-ads-saudi-gcc-benchmark)). That makes it a core channel.

## Merge works, but the market rewards only a few winners

**The genre is growing.** Merge earned **$1.4B in 2025 (+80%)**. Its share of puzzle revenue rose from 14% to 20%. But the **top 10 titles took about 80%** of that money ([AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/casual-report-2025)). Gossip Harbor peaked near **$62M a month** in October 2025 ([GamerBraves on AppMagic](https://www.gamerbraves.com/gossip-harbors-liveops-journey-shows-how-merge-2-scaled-from-20-to-100-monthly-events/)). Smaller titles tell a more realistic story for a small studio. Love & Pies survived five years and rebuilt its whole economy in 2024 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/trailmix-on-five-years-of-love-and-pies-there-is-no-status-quo-in-live-service/)). Merge Mansion "plateaued", and its maker cut **159 jobs** ([GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/merge-mansion-dev-metacore-lays-off-159-employees-closes-studios-in-germany-and-sweden-r3779/)).

**How Gossip Harbor won.** It did not invent a new mechanic. It won in three ways. It used a soap-opera story for women around 32. It made item chains simpler, which gave **about 27% more sessions** than Travel Town ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/gossip-harbors-meteoric-rise-in-merge/)). And it ran the densest event calendar in the genre. It grew from **about 20 events a month to about 50, then nearly 100** ([AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/gossip-harbor-liveops)). Events were mostly reskins. Over 130 days it ran **281 events from only 64 event types**, with **28-day passes** and **40-day album collections** ([Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/gossip-harbor-liveops-cheatsheet/)). About **62% of its in-app revenue came from special offers and bundles** ([Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/gossip-harbor)). Its ads lead with "drama before gameplay" ([CreativeScope](https://www.creativescope.ai/blog/gossip-harbor-ua-teardown-drama-before-gameplay)). This is good news for you. A culturally specific family drama is exactly this kind of hook.

**The common failure.** Players hate energy walls. Late orders cost hundreds of energy against a cap of 100 ([PlayNForge](https://www.playnforge.com/best-merge-games/)). Travel Town's fix: players always return to a board with **20–25% empty space and full energy** ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/why-travel-town-is-dominating-mobile-merge/)).

## The evidence corrects three earlier assumptions

**Dialect: keep Jordanian flavor, but make it light and urban.** Over half of GCC gamers prefer Arabic text and voice. **41%** say they only play, or are more likely to play, Arabic-localized games ([Niko Partners](https://nikopartners.com/speaking-the-language-localization-in-mena/)). But the top complaint about Arabic localization is **"poor dialect choices"** ([Niko x SEF whitepaper](https://nikopartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Speaking-The-Language_Localization-in-the-MENA-Region_SEF-x-Niko-Partners-Whitepaper_English.pdf)). Gulf audiences know Levantine Arabic mostly through Syrian and Lebanese drama ([Arab Media & Society](https://arabmediasociety.com/the-state-of-the-musalsal-arab-television-drama-and-comedy-and-the-politics-of-the-satellite-era/)). Informal sources say Gulf speakers **may find Jordanian or Palestinian dialect harder** than Syrian or Lebanese ([Playaling](https://playaling.com/mutual-intelligibility-of-arabic-dialects/)). That source is a blog, so this is a risk to test, not a fact. In a study of 613 Arab gamers, about **64% preferred Arabic UI and text with subtitled audio**, and only 24% wanted full dubbing ([JICC review](https://immi.se/index.php/intercultural/article/view/10.36923.jicc.v26i3.1528)). The corrected plan has four parts. Use light MSA or neutral Arabic for menus and system text. Use urban Ammani speech for dialogue, not heavy rural forms. Add a tap-to-explain glossary for very local words. Test comprehension with Saudi readers before launch. Ads are a separate matter. Saudi viewers can tell a creator's dialect instantly, and it affects trust. Use Najdi or Hijazi creators for Saudi ads and Egyptian creators for wide reach ([OneHub](https://onehub.ae/arabic-ugc-creators-for-gcc-ads/)).

**Ramadan: buy users before Ramadan, keep and delight them during it.** The earlier idea was "Ramadan for acquisition". The data is mixed. Adjust reported **game installs +24% in Ramadan and +27% around Eid** (2025 data), with Saudi Arabia leading install growth ([InvestGame on Adjust](https://investgame.net/news/pdf/2026-01-27-ramadan-app-trends-report/)). AppsFlyer, for the same Ramadan, found **gaming flat**: sessions +0.05%, non-organic installs −1%, and IAP conversion steady at 3.26% ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/ramadan-app-revenue/)). Agencies claim ad prices rise **30–60%** in Ramadan, but no one has audited this ([Hovi](https://thehovi.com/blog/agency-insights/google-ads-saudi-arabia-complete-ppc-guide-2026)). Games had the longest Ramadan sessions, at **35.6 minutes** on average ([Campaign Post on Adjust](https://www.campaignpost.net/article/key-mobile-app-trends-and-insights-for-ramadan-2025-adjust)). Saudi women are most active online from **10 PM to 3 AM** in Ramadan ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399432733_Understanding_Nighttime_Digital_Behavior_During_Ramadan_A_Case_Study_on_Saudi_Women)). The better plan has three steps. Scale paid ads 2–4 weeks **before** Ramadan, when prices are normal. Use Ramadan for engagement, nightly story unlocks after iftar, store featuring and retargeting. Use Eid for gifting and bundles. No source measured Eid in-app spending for games, so Eid revenue is still a guess. The same applies to the wedding theme: no evidence was found either way.

**AI voice: use a human voice for Sitti.** Vendors say they offer Saudi and Egyptian voices ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/egyptian-accent-text-to-speech)). But users report problems. In one case a voice cloned from a Lebanese speaker **came out speaking Saudi Arabic** ([Retell AI community](https://community.retellai.com/t/arabic-language-voice-agent-the-client-base-is-mainly-in-the-levant-region-so-i-need-support-for-lebanese-accent/1452)). In another, output drifted to formal Arabic or Egyptian ([Vapi](https://support.vapi.ai/t/27085893/arabic-dialects)). Researchers still treat dialect TTS as an open problem ([ACL Anthology 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/credits/2026.abjadnlp-1.6)). There is also backlash risk. Arc Raiders used paid, consenting actors for its AI voices and was still criticized. In March 2026 its CEO said "a real professional actor is better than AI" and began replacing lines ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/arc-raiders/ai-voices-replaced)). A flat robotic grandmother would damage your main selling point. So hire a native Ammani voice actor for Sitti's key lines. Use a written contract. Copy the consent rules from the SAG-AFTRA 2025 agreement: written consent for any voice copy, a clear scope and pay for replica sessions ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/329479/video-game-performers-ai-consent-rules)). A consented clone of that same actor can cover short event lines later, after native speakers approve it.

**AI art: use it, but not for faces.** Big casual studios use AI for backgrounds, item variants and event art. They keep characters human-made. Rovio cut Season Pass background time by **80% (4 days instead of 20)** and does not use AI for characters ([zenn.dev summary of AWS talk](https://zenn.dev/kiiwami/articles/ab05fb630838f937?locale=en); [Music x Media](https://musicmedia.fi/en/program/art-ai-angry-birds-delivering-on-the-brand-promise-in-the-era-of-generative-ai-tools/)). Playtika targets a **50%** cut in art time ([ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/production-scale-generative-ai-infrastructure-for-game-art-creation)). Playrix fine-tunes FLUX models on its house style ([Remocate](https://www.remocate.app/jobs/generative-ai-engineer)). Purely AI-made art cannot be copyrighted in the US ([USCO](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). That is another reason to keep Sitti, Salma and the logo human-made. In the same survey that found 36% of developers use generative AI, **52%** said it harms the industry ([BusinessWire, GDC 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260129438528/en/2026-State-of-the-Game-Industry-Report-Reveals-Widening-Effect-of-Layoffs-Broader-Perspectives-on-Generative-AI-Unionization-Tariffs-and-More)). Mobile ads made with AI have also drawn backlash ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/activision-gets-criticized-for-ai-generated-mobile-game-advertisements/)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what the next step should be. The concept does not need more ideas on paper. It needs proof of two things that no source can supply. First, will Saudi women, the people who hold most of the money, connect with a Jordanian grandmother's café in Ammani dialect? Second, will they pay without paid random boxes and through store billing? The cheapest way to learn the first answer is a reading test and a fake-door ad test, costing a few thousand dollars per country. That should happen before most of the art is built. The second answer needs a Saudi soft launch.

The strongest new insight is that the concept's edge is not its mechanics. It is cultural authenticity, delivered through a steady stream of reskinned events and human-made characters. AI should cut the cost of the repeated items, backgrounds and event art. It should not touch the faces and voices that players trust. Treat 7.8 as an honest start. Let each sub-score rise only when its gate is passed with real players. That keeps the score useful as a decision tool and not just a hope.
