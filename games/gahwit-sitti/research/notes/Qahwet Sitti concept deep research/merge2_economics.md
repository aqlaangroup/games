## 1. Retention, ARPDAU, payer conversion, LTV and ROAS targets (merge-2 / casual puzzle, 2024–2026)

### Takeaway
No source gives public KPI benchmarks specific to merge-2. For casual puzzle the usual figures are D1 about 30–32%, D7 about 10–14% and D30 about 3–7%, with top titles at 40–50% D1. Payer conversion is commonly cited at 1–5%, and casual 90-day ARPPU at about $7. Most numbers come from vendor blogs with conflicting figures, so they are rough reference points. A merge-2 game with heavy IAP should aim above the puzzle median, because the leaders monetize engaged payers deeply rather than relying on volume.

### Cited Findings
- Puzzle genre retention: D1 31.85%, D7 12.18%, D30 5.35% (vendor genre table, 2026 article; underlying data year unclear) — [Zorka / Playio genre tables via search](https://zorka.agency/blog/13-kpis-to-measure-your-mobile-game); [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/retention-by-game-genre)
- Alternate puzzle range: D1 30–40%, D7 10–20%, D30 3–7% — [Genzopia puzzle stats 2026](https://www.genzopia.com/blog/puzzle-games-statistics-2026)
- GameAnalytics (2025, per secondary source): median D1 across 11,600 games and 1.48B MAU is about 22%, all genres — [Segwise retention benchmarks 2026](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies)
- AppsFlyer (Q3 2022 genre data + Q3 2023 platform split, **old data**): casual and puzzle Android D1 28–32%, D7 9–12%, D30 3.5–5% — [GameGrowthAdvisor retention 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/)
- Top-tier titles regularly reach 40–50% D1 — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies)
- Sensor Tower: the top 25 casual games averaged 14.9% D7 retention in December 2025 — [GameGrowthAdvisor KPIs 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/)
- ARPDAU health floor: above $0.15 for ad-monetized games and above $0.30 for IAP-led games, with strong casual performers around $0.80. **Single source, low confidence** — [GameGrowthAdvisor KPIs 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/)
- Payer conversion: "most games 1–3%" vs "2–5% IAP payers across gaming". Sources conflict, and Playio calls casual-puzzle conversion "unverifiable" in public trackers — [Playio ARPPU](https://blog.playio.co/arppu-mobile-games); [Zorka](https://zorka.agency/blog/13-kpis-to-measure-your-mobile-game)
- AppsFlyer cumulative 90-day IAP ARPPU (Jan 2025–Mar 2026): casual $7.26, midcore $9.80 — [Playio ARPPU](https://blog.playio.co/arppu-mobile-games)
- D7 ROAS: ad-monetized casual about 7–8% D7 and 40–50% D30 on iOS (attributed to Liftoff 2025). Hyper-casual D7 7.6% iOS / 7.8% Android, mid-core 4.3% iOS / 6.1% Android — [Zorka LTV/ROAS](https://zorka.agency/blog/ltv-roas-payback-windows); [Segwise ROAS](https://segwise.ai/blog/benchmark-roas-mobile-games-ad-networks-countries)
- Recommended method: forecast LTV from your own mature cohorts with a D90:D7 (or D180:D7) multiplier, and set break-even D7 ROAS = 1 / multiplier — [Zorka LTV/ROAS](https://zorka.agency/blog/ltv-roas-payback-windows); [Keentics](https://www.keentics.com/blog/calculating-mobile-ltv/)
- Engagement proxy for merge leaders: Gossip Harbor players average about 27% more sessions than Travel Town players and more weekly minutes (Sensor Tower data via Naavik, 2025) — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/gossip-harbors-meteoric-rise-in-merge/)

### Inferences
- Reasonable soft-launch targets for a merge-2 story game (Tier-1 or MENA Tier-1 such as KSA/UAE): D1 ≥ 38–40%, D7 ≥ 15%, D30 ≥ 6–8%. This sits above the puzzle median, because merge-2 meta (story plus decoration) holds engaged users well and the genre leaders monetize long tails.
- Merge-2 is IAP-whale-driven: about 62% of Gossip Harbor IAP comes from special offers and bundles (see §3). The key early signals are payer conversion and ARPPU growth curves (D7 → D30 → D90), not ARPDAU from ads. The usual payback horizon for this genre is long, 180–365+ days. This is an inference, and no source stated a merge-specific payback window.
- Because merge-2 LTV curves keep rising for a long time, D7 ROAS targets will be low, likely in the single digits to low teens. The decision should rest on the D7 → D90/D180 multiplier from your own cohorts.

### Gaps
- No public source gave merge-2-specific D1/D7/D30, ARPDAU, payer conversion or LTV. Microfun, Moon Active and Metacore do not disclose these. Paid GameRefinery, Sensor Tower or AppMagic dashboards, or GameAnalytics genre filters, would be needed.
- No MENA- or Arabic-speaking-audience benchmarks found for merge games.
- I could not verify a specific merge-2 ROAS payback window from a primary source.

## 2. Comparable titles: revenue, downloads, audience, team, development, soft launch

### Takeaway
Merge became the fastest-growing casual puzzle sub-genre in 2025: $1.4B revenue (+80% YoY) per AppMagic, and about 80% of that revenue goes to the top 10 titles. Gossip Harbor (Microfun) took over leadership from Travel Town and Merge Mansion in late 2024. It then reached about $62M/month IAP by October 2025, about one third of the merge segment. Love & Pies and Mergest Kingdom are much smaller. Merge Mansion plateaued, and that plateau, together with failed diversification, drove Metacore's layoffs and its full sale to Supercell.

### Inferences
- The market is winner-take-most. The top 10 take about 80% of revenue, and none of 91 new merge launches in H1 2025 reached $100k/month. A small studio cannot win by out-spending Microfun or Moon Active on US UA. It needs a differentiated, underserved audience: Arabic-speaking women fits, since Gossip Harbor's core is women around 32. It also needs cheaper UA geos with high ARPPU (GCC).
- Merge Mansion's revenue-per-FTE decline is a cautionary lesson about headcount. A lean team built around one game, as Microfun's "lean ops" framing suggests, is more sustainable than scaling staff ahead of revenue.
- Love & Pies (small revenue, years of survival, economy rebuild) is a more realistic "small studio" outcome than Gossip Harbor.

## 3. Why Gossip Harbor overtook rivals (events, economy, narrative, art, UA, LiveOps cadence)

### Takeaway
Gossip Harbor took a proven merge-2 core (same mechanics as Travel Town) and won on three fronts. First, a soap-opera narrative wrapper aimed at women around 32. Second, simpler item progression that drove more sessions. Third, the industry's densest LiveOps calendar, growing from about 20 events/month (2022–23) to about 50 (2024) to nearly 100 (Q3 2025), with offers woven into the event flow. Its UA follows the same "drama before gameplay" approach at very high creative volume.

### Cited Findings
- Core mechanics are standard merge-2. The main difference from Travel Town is the focus on narrative rather than light city-building. Ads lean toward emotional and controversial themes, and live ops use "reskinned events to ensure enough novelty" — [Deconstructor of Fun 2024](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2024/8/19/finding-genre-success-the-case-of-gossip-harbor)
- About 27% more sessions per player than Travel Town and more weekly minutes, attributed to simpler item progression — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/gossip-harbors-meteoric-rise-in-merge/)
- Event concurrency: a minimum of 4–5 concurrent events and 8–10 at most. Longer-duration seasonal PvP, PvE and decoration events vs Travel Town — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/gossip-harbors-meteoric-rise-in-merge/)
- LiveOps cadence: about 20 events/month early on, most lasting 1–10 days. In Q2–Q3 2024 the count rose to about 50/month: daily launches went from about 8–10 to 20+ per month, and short events roughly doubled from about 10 to about 25. Nearly 100 events/month in Q3 2025. Events began to "form chains, with one loop feeding into the next". Monetization was "tied to event flow so spending felt like part of play" — [AppMagic LiveOps research](https://appmagic.rocks/research/gossip-harbor-liveops); [GamerBraves](https://www.gamerbraves.com/gossip-harbors-liveops-journey-shows-how-merge-2-scaled-from-20-to-100-monthly-events/); [Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/gossip-harbors-liveops/)
- Market context: the AppMagic LiveOps report puts the average at 89 events/month by end of 2025 (comparison set per that report) — [AppMagic LiveOps Report 2025 summary](https://mobidictum.com/appmagic-liveops-report-2025/); [InvestGame PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-15-LiveOps_Report_2025_EN.pdf)
- Gamigion's 130-day calendar sample: 281 event instances, **64 unique event types**, an average of 8.6 live events per day (peak 16). "Flaming Frenzy" ran 91 times. **4 paid pass seasons of 28 days each** and **3 collection (album) arcs of 40 days each** — [Gamigion LiveOps cheatsheet](https://www.gamigion.com/gossip-harbor-liveops-cheatsheet/)
- Named event formats: Race (one of the first in regular rotation, 2023), Album/collection events (adopted early), Lava Quests, Win Streak, Flaming Frenzy, battle/season pass, seasonal PvP/PvE, decoration events — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/gossip-harbor-liveops-cheatsheet/); [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/gossip-harbor-liveops); [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/gossip-harbors-meteoric-rise-in-merge/)
- LiveOps start in the first sessions. Events and offers are part of onboarding, and by the early-mid levels multiple events run in parallel (Balancy webinar breakdown) — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/how-gossip-harbor-builds-live-ops-from-day-one/)
- About 62% of IAP comes from special offers and limited bundles — [Udonis (AppMagic data)](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/gossip-harbor)
- UA: more than 9,000 ads/week, about 2,800 unique creatives, under one third of output is new, under 10% of budget goes to experiments — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/match-3-is-over-merge-won-gossip-harbor-makes-34m-a-month/). Ads lead with emotional stakes ("drama before gameplay") — [CreativeScope](https://www.creativescope.ai/blog/gossip-harbor-ua-teardown-drama-before-gameplay). Concentration: one 25-second video got about 70.5% of delivery in Admapix's 2026 sample, which conflicts with the "high volume" picture — [Admapix](https://www.admapix.com/blog/ad-intelligence/gossip-harbor-ad-strategy-2026)
- Microfun tests several merge-adjacent concepts, each aimed at a different audience — [Sett newsletter](https://www.sett.ai/content/unpredictable-hits-newsletter-003/)

### Inferences
- Gossip Harbor's edge is not a new mechanic. It comes from audience-fit narrative, low cognitive load in item chains, and LiveOps density built on reusable, reskinnable event templates. Qahwet Sitti should build an event framework (race, album, pass, streak, milestone and frenzy templates) before content, so that cadence can scale cheaply.
- A small studio cannot run 100 events per month at launch. Gossip Harbor itself started at about 20/month, which is a realistic launch target.
- "Drama-first" UA translates directly to culturally specific family and neighborhood drama, such as gossip at Sitti's café, but it needs cultural sensitivity review for GCC audiences.

### Gaps
- Gossip Harbor's exact story chapter cadence, item and chain counts and art pipeline: not found.
- No primary Microfun interview found. The PocketGamer.biz piece is a third-party (Balancy) breakdown.

## 4. Typical content cadence for merge games and LiveOps team size

### Takeaway
Public data on event cadence exists (about 20–100 events/month for leaders, an 89/month market average by end of 2025, 28-day passes, 40-day albums). Public data on story chapters, items, merge chains and LiveOps team headcount is essentially absent from the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- Event cadence benchmarks: Gossip Harbor about 20 → 50 → nearly 100 events/month (2022 → 2024 → Q3 2025). AppMagic's market average was 89/month at end of 2025 — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/gossip-harbor-liveops); [Mobidictum LiveOps report](https://mobidictum.com/appmagic-liveops-report-2025/)
- Gossip Harbor: about 8.6 concurrent events/day, a 28-day pass season and a 40-day album arc. Its 64 event types came from about 281 instances over 130 days, showing heavy reuse ("Flaming Frenzy" 91×) — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/gossip-harbor-liveops-cheatsheet/)
- Travel Town merge design: nested and combined chains, plus a Power Boost toggle that doubles generator energy use — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/deconstructing-magmatic-games-travel-town/); [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/travel-town-deconstruction-merge-2-whales/)
- Love & Pies: five years of continuous live service, with an economy rebuild after four years — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/trailmix-on-five-years-of-love-and-pies-there-is-no-status-quo-in-live-service/)
- Metacore (Merge Mansion) had 223 staff in 2024 for essentially one live game plus R&D. This is an upper bound and not LiveOps-specific — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/is-metacore-a-warning-sign/)

### Inferences
- A practical launch cadence for a small studio: one 28-day pass, one 30–40-day album/collection, two to four short events per week (race, streak, frenzy reskins), one themed seasonal event per month (Ramadan, Eid, National Days, Mother's Day; regional inference), and a new story chapter or area every 2–4 weeks. This is derived from the Gossip Harbor structure scaled down, not a cited benchmark.
- Event reuse ratio is the cost lever. Gossip Harbor runs about 4.4 instances per unique type in 130 days, so reskins rather than new mechanics drive cadence.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures for story chapters per month, new items or chains per month, or LiveOps team headcount at any merge studio.

## 5. Common failures of merge games and how successful games solved them

### Takeaway
The recurring failure modes are energy walls (late-game orders costing hundreds of energy against a 100 cap), economy drift over years, content droughts, revenue plateaus, and launching into a saturated top-heavy market. Successful titles reduce friction at session start (Travel Town's open board with refilled energy), monetize energy through optional boosts and offers, scale reusable events, and rebuild economies when needed (Love & Pies, Merge Mansion's 2024 rebound).

### Cited Findings
- Energy friction: players complain that later orders "take a ton of energy" while still capped at 100. Merge Mansion regenerates 1 energy every 2 minutes, so "you can't play a ton at once". Advanced Travel Town orders take hundreds of energy — [Google Play reviews/dev reply](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=io.randomco.travel&hl=en_US); [PlayNForge roundup 2026](https://www.playnforge.com/best-merge-games/)
- Travel Town's session-start fix: players always return to a board with 20–25% empty space and refilled energy, so the first merge happens within seconds. Energy is the main monetization lever (Power Boost doubles energy use for faster output) — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/why-travel-town-is-dominating-mobile-merge/)
- Item-progression simplicity raised sessions in Gossip Harbor (+27% vs Travel Town) — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/gossip-harbors-meteoric-rise-in-merge/)
- Economy drift: Love & Pies rebuilt its whole economy in 2024 after the core economy "had not fundamentally changed since launch" — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/trailmix-on-five-years-of-love-and-pies-there-is-no-status-quo-in-live-service/)
- Revenue dip and recovery: Merge Mansion fell from $11.4M (December 2023) to about $9M (February 2024), then recovered to $11.97M (December 2024) through LiveOps and monetization changes — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/merge-mansion-reclaiming-top-spot)
- Plateau and studio risk: Merge Mansion "plateaued", Metacore's diversification failed to deliver a second global title, and the company cut 159 jobs. Commentators also cite high UA costs and Chinese competition — [GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/merge-mansion-dev-metacore-lays-off-159-employees-closes-studios-in-germany-and-sweden-r3779/); [Undrads](https://www.undrads.com/blogs/supercell-acquires-metacore-merge-mansion)
- Market saturation: none of 91 merge launches in H1 2025 exceeded $100k monthly net revenue — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/casual-report-h1-2025)
- Players accept energy as normal for the genre, but "great games let you progress without forcing a purchase at every wall" (consumer guide) — [PlayNForge](https://www.playnforge.com/best-merge-games/)

### Inferences
- Design guardrails: tune order energy cost against regen and cap so a non-payer can finish at least one meaningful goal per session. Give daily free energy through events and rewarded ads. Make event rewards energy and generator-heavy so LiveOps relieves friction. Plan an economy-audit cadence from day one.
- Avoid content droughts by building a story and area pipeline plus event templates before launch, so cadence does not depend on new mechanics.

## 6. Soft-launch KPI gates publishers use to greenlight a casual game globally

### Takeaway
Typical gates for casual games, sourced mostly from vendor and agency guides: D1 35–40%+, D7 ≥ 15%, D30 ≥ 6%, crash rate under 1%, and projected LTV above CPI with a clear path to payback. A D1 below about 25% on Android is a kill signal. Cohorts need about 900+ installs to be read reliably. Tier-1 casual Android CPI is about $1.20–3.50 in the US.

### Cited Findings
- Global-launch readiness: D1 ideally over 40%, crash rate under 1%, LTV above CPI — [AppAgent soft vs global launch](https://appagent.com/blog/soft-launch-vs-global-launch/)

Read cohorts only at about 900+ installs (a D1 near 30% needs about 896 installs for ±3 points at 95% confidence) — [GameGrowthAdvisor soft-launch guide](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2025-12-16-mobile-soft-launch-complete-guide/); [game-developers.org soft-launch gates](https://game-developers.org/soft-launch-gates-the-data-driven-playbook-to-stop-scaling-leaky-games)
