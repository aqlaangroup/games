# Arab Women Mobile Gamers (2024–2026): Who They Are, What They Play and Pay For, How Countries Differ

> Method note: These notes come from WebSearch result summaries. WebFetch was blocked in this session for nikopartners.com, mdpi.com and campaignme.com (DNS/network allowlist), so primary PDFs (Niko Partners MENA-3 report, Niko x SEF localization whitepaper, Niko x Xsolla monetization whitepaper, MDPI/ACM Saudi women papers) could not be opened in full. Numbers below are as reported in search snippets of those primary pages; anything taken from third-party blogs is flagged **[secondary/unverified]**. Older data is labeled with its year.

## 1. What share of gamers are women in Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt, Jordan and the wider Levant/GCC, and how is it trending?

### Takeaway
The best 2025 figure is Niko Partners' survey: women are **37%+ of gamers in MENA-3 (Saudi, UAE, Egypt combined)**, up from 32.6% in 2023, in markets that once skewed over 80% male. Per-country figures conflict sharply depending on definition (any mobile play vs. "gamer" vs. esports audience). No reliable 2024–2026 figure exists for Jordan or the Levant.

### Cited Findings
- **MENA-3 (2025):** Women account for 37% of gamers in MENA-3 (Saudi, UAE, Egypt), vs. 40% in India; Niko describes MENA-3 as markets that "traditionally skewed over 80% male" — [Niko Partners, Asia & MENA market model 2025](https://nikopartners.com/asia-mena-market-model-2025/); [Niko Partners 2026 predictions](https://nikopartners.com/niko-partners-2026-predictions/)
- **Trend:** Women were "more than 37%" of the MENA-3 player base in Niko's 2025 survey (n≈1,580 gamers in Egypt, KSA, UAE), up from **32.6% in 2023** — [Niko Partners, MENA Market 2025](https://nikopartners.com/the-future-of-gaming-in-mena-3-understanding-the-opportunity/); [Arab Game News on Niko](https://www.arabgamenews.com/post/mena-gaming-next-phase-niko-partners)
- **Forecast:** Niko predicts women will make up over 40% of the games market across Asia and MENA-3 — [Niko Partners 2026 predictions](https://nikopartners.com/niko-partners-2026-predictions/)
- **Saudi Arabia, government figure (date unclear):** Ministry of Communications and IT (MCIT) figures say **48% of the Kingdom's gamers are women**, out of 23.5 million gamers (~70% of population) — [Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/624188); also repeated by [ITP.live](https://itp.live/news/the-surprising-rise-of-saudi-women-gamers-at-48) and [AboutHer](https://www.abouther.com/node/52066/people/features/almost-half-saudi-arabias-gamers-are-women). The original survey year could not be confirmed.
- **Saudi Arabia, esports-oriented survey (Dec 2024):** Power League Gaming found women ≈**27% of Saudi gamers and 20% of UAE gamers**. This survey focused on esports awareness and viewership, which likely skews male — [CABSAT on PLG study](https://cabsat.com/saudi-arabia-emerges-as-leading-force-in-global-esports-study-reveals)
- **Saudi device preference:** A Gaming Analytics survey reported by Campaign Middle East found **61% of Saudi female gamers prefer smartphones**, 24% consoles, 15% PC (date unclear) — [Campaign Middle East](https://campaignme.com/test-and-learn-approach-to-saudi-female-gamer-boom/)
- **Saudi (2021, older):** AdColony/Nielsen put Saudi mobile gamers at 55% male / 45% female. A later AdColony/On Device study said **90% of Saudi women** called themselves mobile gamers vs. 83% of men — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/a-deep-dive-into-saudi-arabias-mobile-gamers-1960866); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/highlights-from-research-on-mobile-gaming-in-the-middle-east/)
- **UAE (older, ~2017–2018):** AdColony/On Device: 84% of UAE women and 87% of men play on mobile — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/going-out/society/over-80-of-uae-residents-are-mobile-gamers-shows-survey-1.2228746). Wikipedia cites a study that 44% of UAE women play video games (original source not verified) — [Wikipedia: Esports in the UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esports_in_the_United_Arab_Emirates)
- **Egypt (older, ~2018):** AdColony/On Device data conflict. One summary gives 93% of men and 89% of women as mobile gamers, another 90% of women and 83% of men — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/highlights-from-research-on-mobile-gaming-in-the-middle-east/); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/mobile-gaming-in-middle-east-under-the-microscope-704495)
- **Jordan/Levant (2018, old):** Northwestern Qatar's *Media Use in the Middle East* found Jordanians, Egyptians and Lebanese were the least likely nationals in the region to play video games. Only **38% of Jordanians** played on a phone. Region-wide, 56% of men and 48% of women played on phones, and mobile gaming was skewing older and more female — [Media Use in the Middle East 2018](https://www.mideastmedia.org/survey/2018/chapter/gaming/)
- **Global benchmark (2025):** ESA's 21-country survey (includes UAE) found 47–48% of players are women, with mobile the preferred device for women (64%) — [NeoGAF summary of ESA 2025](https://www.neogaf.com/threads/entertainment-software-association-esa-2025-global-video-games-report-48-of-video-game-players-are-women-avg-age-41-55-prefer-mobile-and-more.1688931/) **[secondary summary]**

### Inferences
- The figures conflict because they measure different things. "Any mobile play" (Saudi MCIT 48%, AdColony ~45–50%) captures casual mobile players like the Qahwet Sitti audience. "Self-identified gamer" or esports surveys (Niko 37%, PLG 27%) undercount them. For a casual merge/story title, women are realistically **close to half of mobile players in KSA** and probably a large share in Egypt and UAE too.
- The trend is clearly upward in every source, from 32.6% in 2023 to 37%+ in 2025 (Niko).
- Jordan is the weakest-evidenced market. Its 2018 phone-gaming rate (38%) was low, but that data is 8 years old.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 gender split for Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, Palestine, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain or Oman was found.
- No per-country 2025 gender split within MENA-3 was found publicly; the Niko report likely has one, but the domain was blocked.
- The year and method of the Saudi MCIT 48% figure are unconfirmed.

## 2. Which genres, themes and mechanics do Arab women play most? Any titles named as favorites?

### Takeaway
Academic work on Saudi women (2025–2026) consistently finds a preference for **casual, narrative-rich, mobile, social** games with escapism, creativity and identity expression as motivations. Puzzle is the #1 mobile genre in MENA-3 overall. No source gives Arab-women-specific rankings of merge, decor, fashion or cooking games, or names their favorite titles.

### Cited Findings
- **Saudi women profile (ACM CHI PLAY 2025 work-in-progress, n=247 women + 5 interviews):** Saudi women gamers are "socially driven, emotionally immersed, creatively expressive". They are "primarily driven by social connection and emotional immersion, with a strong preference for casual, narrative-rich, and mobile games" — [ACM DL: The Surprising Gaming Profile of Saudi Women](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3744736.3749348); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394251414_The_Surprising_Gaming_Profile_of_Saudi_Women)
- In that sample, **82% of women identified as casual gamers** and only 9% as serious gamers. The authors argue this may be "strategic gender performance" rather than lower interest — [ResearchGate (Saudi gamers study)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400261823_What_Drives_Saudi_Gamers_A_Study_of_Gender_Genre_and_Geography)
- Saudi women prefer genres "that allow for creativity, sociality, and identity experimentation". Their listed favorite genres were **multiplayer, puzzle and open world** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400261823_What_Drives_Saudi_Gamers_A_Study_of_Gender_Genre_and_Geography)
- **"What Drives Saudi Gamers?" (MDPI Behavioral Sciences, 2026, n=397, 62.2% female, non-representative):** Men report more performance and competition motives, while women show stronger preferences for **escapism and casual gameplay**. Women were 53% casual self-identifiers vs. 22% of men (one copy appears to reverse these figures, so verify before citing) — [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16020202)
- **MENA-3 genre (2025, all genders):** Puzzle is the #1 mobile genre, with sports #2 — [Niko Partners MENA Market 2025](https://nikopartners.com/the-future-of-gaming-in-mena-3-understanding-the-opportunity/)
- **Saudi genre (older, all genders):** AdColony/Nielsen 2021 ranked battle royale first, then action/adventure, then puzzle/trivia/word. A Newzoo-based summary listed puzzles at 39%, sports 34%, racing 31%, adventure 29% — [Arab News 2021](https://www.arabnews.com/media/a-deep-dive-into-saudi-arabias-mobile-gamers-1960866); [INTENTA](https://intenta.digital/gaming-industry/video-game-industry-saudi-arabia/) **[secondary]**
- **What engages women (Niko, Asia & MENA, 2026):** narrative-driven games, live-service models, cosmetics/customization, and influencer/streamer marketing. Niko cites women-oriented hits like *Love and Deepspace* topping revenue charts in Asia — [Niko Partners: Rise of women-oriented gaming in Asia](https://nikopartners.com/from-otome-hits-to-competitive-play-the-rise-of-women-oriented-gaming-in-asia/)
- **Saudi market charts (2025):** Sensor Tower's Saudi category pages show Hay Day in the tycoon top 5 (Android, Q2 2025) — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-android-top-5-tycoon%20games-revenue-sa-602ae798241bc16eb86ec5ed). Royal Match was #4 worldwide top-grossing in June 2025 (global, not regional) — [Outlook Respawn on Sensor Tower](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/mobile-games-82-billion-revenue-2025-sensor-tower-report)
- **Global context:** In a Mistplay survey, 84% of puzzle gamers say they seek relaxation and 81% are women — [adjoe](https://adjoe.io/glossary/puzzle-games-mobile/) **[secondary]**. Statista: women spend 19.1 min/day on mobile puzzle vs. 17.6 for men (global 2024) — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1395578/global-daily-mobile-gaming-engagement-puzzle-gender/)

### Inferences
- The Qahwet Sitti mix (merge puzzle + narrative + café makeover/decoration) lines up directly with the documented Saudi women motivations: casual play, narrative and emotional immersion, creativity and identity expression, and escapism. A social layer (for example gifting, or visiting friends' cafés) addresses the "socially driven" finding without needing open voice chat (see section 5).
- Puzzle being the #1 MENA-3 mobile genre supports the merge core.

### Gaps
- No MENA data broken down by gender for merge, match-3, decor, fashion, cooking or social casino.
- No survey names favorite titles for Arab women. Candy Crush, Royal Match, Gardenscapes, Ludo Star, Jawaker and Baloot-type card games are plausible but **unverified**. Sensor Tower/data.ai Saudi puzzle and casino category pages were not found in search.

## 3. How much do they spend, how do they pay, and how does spending differ between Saudi, UAE, Egypt and Jordan?

### Takeaway
Spending per player is very uneven: **UAE ≈ $85, Saudi ≈ $55, Egypt ≈ $3.40 ARPU (2024)**. Payment habits differ too. Saudi players rely on Mada, Egypt on mobile wallets and carrier billing (low card use), and the UAE resembles Western markets. No women-specific spending data was found for any Arab country.

### Cited Findings
- **ARPU 2024:** UAE ≈ **$84.60**, Saudi Arabia ≈ **$54.89**, Egypt ≈ **$3.39** — [Niko Partners MENA Market 2025](https://nikopartners.com/the-future-of-gaming-in-mena-3-understanding-the-opportunity/) (via search summary)
- **Market size:** MENA-3 ≈ $2.15B in 2025 (+7.1%), forecast at $2.31B in 2026 and $2.94B by 2030 — [Niko Partners MENA Market 2025](https://nikopartners.com/the-future-of-gaming-in-mena-3-understanding-the-opportunity/). GCC player spending forecast at $3.2B by 2028 — [Niko Partners news](https://nikopartners.com/news/a-strategic-gamechanger-arabic-localization-identified-as-a-top-priority-for-game-developers-with-gcc-total-player-spending-set-to-reach-3-2-billion-by-2028/)
- **Egypt's scale/revenue gap:** Egypt has more gamers than Saudi Arabia and the UAE combined but brings in about 7% of MENA-3 revenue — [Forestry Games blog](https://shop.forestrygames.com/blog/egypt-more-gamers-than-saudi-and-uae-combined-7-percent-of-revenue) **[secondary/unverified]**. Egypt also leads on engagement at 11.8 hrs/week vs. a 10.8 MENA-3 average (2025) — [FocusGN on Niko](https://focusgn.com/africa/egypt-tops-regional-gaming-charts-with-highest-weekly-engagement)
- **Payment methods (Niko x Xsolla whitepaper, via summaries):** 67% of the MENA population is unbanked or underbanked. Credit card penetration among gamers is **Egypt 2.8%, Saudi 25.4%, UAE 26.8%**. In Saudi Arabia, **Mada is used by 93% of gamers** and PayPal is more widely adopted than credit cards. In Egypt, **Vodafone Cash is used by 74%** of gamers for in-game purchases. Only the UAE resembles Western payment infrastructure — [Xsolla x Niko Partners whitepaper (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Xsolla-Niko-Partners-What-Does-the-Monetization-Landscape-Look-Like-for-Video-Games-in-MENA.pdf); per-country percentages via [Virtwave](https://www.virtwave.com/blog/middle-east-gaming-statistics-2026) **[secondary; verify against the whitepaper]**
- **Web shops:** More than 53% of paying gamers in MENA-3 have bought through official websites or web shops rather than app stores — [Virtwave](https://www.virtwave.com/blog/middle-east-gaming-statistics-2026) **[secondary/unverified]**
- **Carrier billing:** Google Play direct carrier billing covers STC, Mobily, Zain and Virgin (KSA), du and e&/Etisalat (UAE), and Vodafone and e& (Egypt) — [salmangul.com](https://salmangul.com/local-payments-carrier-billing-gaming-gcc/) **[secondary blog]**
- **Saudi IAP growth:** +14% in 2024 among Sensor Tower's tracked frontier markets — [Sensor Tower State of Mobile 2025](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/sensor_tower__state_of_mobile_2025__en_unlocked.pdf)
- **Gender and spend (global, 2023):** Men are about 20% more likely than women to make in-app purchases — [Fluent](https://fluentco.com/blog/2023-gaming-app-insights/) (not MENA-specific)

### Inferences
- For monetization, KSA and UAE are the IAP markets. Egypt and probably Jordan are scale and ad-revenue markets. A café-makeover game should plan **rewarded ads + low-price IAP** for Egypt/Jordan and **cosmetic bundles/battle passes** for the Gulf.
- Supporting Mada (via Apple Pay/STC Pay), carrier billing and a web shop matters more than in Western launches.

### Gaps
- No women-specific ARPU, payer rate or spend data for any Arab country.
- No Jordan ARPU or payment-method data. Jordan is not in MENA-3 reporting; it may appear in Niko's broader MENA coverage, but that was not accessible.

## 4. Language and dialect preferences: Arabic vs English UI, MSA vs dialect, and how Gulf audiences receive Levantine or Egyptian dialect content

### Takeaway
About half of GCC gamers prefer Arabic, and over 40% say they only play, or are more likely to play, Arabic-localized games. Studies favor an Arabic UI plus text with subtitled audio, and MSA for formal voicing, but players complain most about **poor dialect choices**. Gulf audiences have long watched Syrian/Levantine drama. Jordanian dialect specifically is less familiar to Gulf ears than Syrian or Lebanese (informal evidence only).

### Cited Findings
- **GCC (Niko x Saudi Esports Federation, Aug 2024, n≈1,225, surveyed Dec 2023):** "Over half of gamers in the GCC region have a preference for Arabic text and voice localizations" over the original language. **41%** of regional gamers say they only play Arabic-localized games or are more likely to play them — [Niko Partners: Speaking the Language](https://nikopartners.com/speaking-the-language-localization-in-mena/); [SPA](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2155761)
- A later Niko survey found **52%** of players prefer Arabic when given all language options — [Niko Partners news](https://nikopartners.com/news/a-strategic-gamechanger-arabic-localization-identified-as-a-top-priority-for-game-developers-with-gcc-total-player-spending-set-to-reach-3-2-billion-by-2028/)
- Respondents' criticisms of localizations **centered on poor dialect choices** (no percentage given) — [Niko Partners whitepaper PDF](https://nikopartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Speaking-The-Language_Localization-in-the-MENA-Region_SEF-x-Niko-Partners-Whitepaper_English.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Al-Batineh & Alawneh (2022), n=613 Arab gamers:** about 64% preferred **partial localization** (Arabic UI and text with subtitled audio), 24% full dubbing, and 12.1% no localization. A secondary summary says **68.51%** chose MSA for re-voicing (not verified against the paper) — [JICC scoping review](https://immi.se/index.php/intercultural/article/view/10.36923.jicc.v26i3.1528); [UAEU research portal](https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/current-trends-in-localizing-video-games-into-arabic-localization/)
- **Arab mobile gamers study (n=382):** asked about localized media vs. colloquial dialects; the split was not available in snippets — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383969684_Arabic_mobile_game_localizations_Gamer_profiles_preferences_and_implications_for_an_immersive_gaming_experience)
- **Dialect research gap:** the 2025 scoping review says dialect-level adaptation is "largely unaddressed" in the literature — [JICC](https://immi.se/index.php/intercultural/article/view/10.36923.jicc.v26i3.1528). A Saudi-dialect game-localization dataset (SauDial) now exists — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12545822/)
- **Gulf reception of Levantine drama (scholarly essay):** Gulf audiences adopted Syrian shows as an alternative to Egyptian ones. Syrian actors had done "bedouin" dramas for decades, Levantine guest workers in the Gulf "probably rendered the Syrian Arabic dialect acceptable", and Gulf stations funded and bought Syrian shows — [Arab Media & Society](https://arabmediasociety.com/the-state-of-the-musalsal-arab-television-drama-and-comedy-and-the-politics-of-the-satellite-era/)
- Damascus Arabic is prestigious and widely recognized in Lebanon, Palestine and Jordan through Syrian broadcast media. *Bab Al-Hara* peaked at around 50M views per episode — [Wikipedia: Damascus Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus_Arabic)
- **Informal, learner-blog evidence:** Egyptian is the most widely understood dialect, thanks to film, TV and music. Levantine is second, helped by Syrian/Lebanese drama. Younger Gulf speakers are familiar with Lebanese/Syrian, but **may find Palestinian or Jordanian harder** than those — [Playaling](https://playaling.com/mutual-intelligibility-of-arabic-dialects/); [Lingualism](https://lingualism.com/blogs/the-lingualism-blog/levantine-vs-egyptian-arabic-which-dialect-should-you-study) **[secondary/unverified blogs]**

### Inferences
- An Arabic-first UI is essential, and with no voice-over (text-based story) Qahwet Sitti avoids the dubbing debate. A practical approach is UI and system text in light MSA or neutral Arabic, with **character dialogue in Jordanian dialect** as the flavor. A glossary or tooltip for very local words would help Gulf and Egyptian readers.
- Levantine has drama-driven legitimacy in the Gulf, but most of that exposure is Syrian or Lebanese. Strongly Jordanian/rural vocabulary (e.g., heavy Fallahi or Bedouin-Jordanian forms) may be less transparent. Urban Ammani speech, which sits close to general Levantine, is the safer register.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on gamer preference for dialect vs. MSA in text-based story games, or on women specifically.
- No study of Gulf reception of Jordanian-dialect content in particular, whether drama or games.
- No data on what share of Arab women set their phone or game UI to English vs. Arabic.

## 5. Play habits: time of day, session length, Ramadan behavior, solo vs social, privacy concerns

### Takeaway
MENA-3 gamers play about 11 hours a week, with Egypt highest. Ramadan raises game sessions (especially in KSA/UAE) and shifts activity late at night, after iftar. Saudi women report using strong **privacy self-protection**: they avoid voice chat, hide identity and faces, and stay silent in multiplayer, because of gender-based harassment.

### Cited Findings
- **Weekly play (2025):** MENA-3 average 10.8 hrs/week, Egypt 11.8 hrs/week — [FocusGN on Niko](https://focusgn.com/africa/egypt-tops-regional-gaming-charts-with-highest-weekly-engagement)
- **Ramadan 2025 (Adjust data):** app sessions +7% across METAP, **Saudi +15%**. Games had the longest sessions, averaging **35.6 minutes** during Ramadan — [Campaign Post on Adjust Ramadan 2025](https://www.campaignpost.net/article/key-mobile-app-trends-and-insights-for-ramadan-2025-adjust)
- **Ramadan 2024:** game installs +5% in METAP (UAE +17%, KSA +11%), game sessions +3% (UAE +12%) — [Omnia](https://www.omnia.ae/insights/2025/02/26/ramadan-app-trends) (dating ambiguous)
- **Ramadan 2026:** Adjust benchmarks report exists — [Adjust Ramadan 2026 report PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-27-ramadan-app-trends-report.pdf) (contents not retrieved)
- **Saudi women at night in Ramadan (2025, n=300):** all groups were most active online **10 PM–3 AM**. This covers general digital behavior, not gaming — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399432733_Understanding_Nighttime_Digital_Behavior_During_Ramadan_A_Case_Study_on_Saudi_Women)
- Marketers cite post-iftar and late-night engagement peaks — [BYYD](https://www.byyd.me/en/blog/2025/02/ramadan-marketing-mena/) **[secondary]**
- **Privacy/voice chat (Saudi women, 2025, n=247):** many **avoid voice chat or hide their identity** to avoid gender-based hostility, and many prefer silence in multiplayer. Concealing faces or real identities was linked to harassment and stereotyping. The paper cites an earlier finding that nearly 1 in 5 young Saudi gamers had experienced online harassment. Participants also reported **gatekeeping** that questions their legitimacy as gamers — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3744736.3749348)
- **Harassment prevalence:** a 2022 Arab Journal of Media and Communication Research study found **68.3%** of its sample reported verbal harassment while gaming — cited via [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3744736.3749348) search summary. An ARIJ investigation (2024) found women and girls harassed in voice chat rooms and companies failing to protect them — [ARIJ](https://en.arij.net/gaming-companies-fail-to-protect-women-gamers-from-harassment/)
- **Wider online safety:** 49% of women internet users in Arab States do not feel safe from online harassment — [UN Women Arab States 2022](https://arabstates.unwomen.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/D3_EVAW%20report2572022.pdf)
- **Global:** 59% of women gamers use non-gendered or male identities to avoid harassment (Reach3/Lenovo, not Arab-specific) — [Dazed MENA](https://www.dazed.me/life-culture/virtually-everywhere-inside-the-rise-of-iraqi-gamer-girls)

### Inferences
- Design implications: support **solo-first play with asynchronous, low-exposure social features** (gifting, café visits, leaderboards with nicknames), with no open voice or text chat with strangers. Use **non-photographic avatars** (illustrated, optional hijab or customizable) and no requirement to show a face.
- Ramadan should be a major live-ops beat. Plan late-night events (post-iftar to suhoor) and treat iftar and suhoor café themes as content.
- Shared family devices are plausible but undocumented. A discreet profile option, or not surfacing purchase notifications, could help, but this is unverified.

### Gaps
- No Arab-women-specific data on session length, time of day outside Ramadan, or daily sessions.
- No data on family device sharing among Arab women gamers.

## 6. Qualitative evidence of what Arab women want from games or dislike about current games

### Takeaway
The qualitative signal points to wanting **emotional and narrative immersion, creativity and self-expression, social connection on their own terms, and escapism**. Dislikes center on **harassment and gatekeeping in multiplayer spaces** and **bad Arabic localization, especially dialect choices**. No study asks about cultural representation of Arab women in casual games directly.

### Cited Findings
- Wants: Saudi women are "socially driven, emotionally immersed, creatively expressive", prefer genres that allow "creativity, sociality, and identity experimentation", and are motivated by escapism — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3744736.3749348); [MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16020202)
- Dislikes: gatekeeping and gender-based hostility from male players, bullying in multiplayer — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3744736.3749348); voice-chat harassment and weak platform protections — [ARIJ](https://en.arij.net/gaming-companies-fail-to-protect-women-gamers-from-harassment/); sexist remarks after speaking in voice chat (Kuwait player interview) — [Kuwait Times](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/9722/kuwait/other-news/toxicity-in-gaming-in-the-middle-east/)
- Localization complaints center on poor dialect choices (all genders) — [Niko Partners whitepaper](https://nikopartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Speaking-The-Language_Localization-in-the-MENA-Region_SEF-x-Niko-Partners-Whitepaper_English.pdf)
- Women respond to narrative, cosmetics/customization and influencer marketing (Asia & MENA) — [Niko Partners](https://nikopartners.com/from-otome-hits-to-competitive-play-the-rise-of-women-oriented-gaming-in-asia/)
- Campaign Middle East frames Saudi female gamers as a market that brands should approach with "test and learn" — [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/test-and-learn-approach-to-saudi-female-gamer-boom/)

### Inferences
- A warm, culturally specific, low-toxicity space (a grandmother's café in Amman, story-driven, decoration as self-expression) addresses most of the documented wants and avoids the main dislikes.
- Culturally authentic Arabic writing in a well-chosen dialect could set the game apart, since bad dialect localization is the top complaint about existing games.

### Gaps
- No Arabic-language qualitative studies, focus groups or app-review analyses of women's views on casual/merge/decor games were found.
- No evidence on reactions to depictions of hijab, modest fashion, family themes or romance in games aimed at Arab women.
- Most qualitative evidence is Saudi-only (n=247 and n=397, both convenience samples). There is none for Jordan, Egypt or the UAE.
- Full-text access to the Niko, Xsolla, MDPI, ACM and Campaign ME sources would allow verification. Those domains need to be allowed in the session network settings.
